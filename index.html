2. Can next generation CMB experiments discriminate between $Λ$CDM and dynamical dark energy? A Bayesian forecast for Simons Observatory and CMB-S4
Anna Chiara Ferri, Ruchika, Alessandro Melchiorri

TLDR: Our results quantify the discriminating power of future CMB surveys in a Bayesian framework, and highlight the role of low redshift distance probes in establishing or rejecting a dynamical dark energy scenario. (sumy fallback)

The combination of Planck CMB data with BAO measurements from the second data release of DESI has revealed a statistically significant preference for a time evolving dark energy equation of state parameterized in the Chevallier Polarski Linder (CPL) form. When type Ia supernovae are included, the preference reaches a significance of $4.2σ$ with DESY5, $3.8σ$ with Union3 and $2.8σ$ with Pantheon+. These hint at a departure from $Λ$CDM model. Our previous Fisher matrix analysis suggested that future CMB experiments could probe such a scenario without external low redshift data sets. Here we extend that study by performing a full Bayesian MCMC forecast for the Simons Observatory (SO) and CMB-S4 experiments, assuming as fiducial the DESI+CMB best fit in the $w_0 w_a$CDM model. We generate mock power spectra with the Cobaya mock CMB pipeline. To quantify the geometric degeneracy in the CMB between $w_0, w_a$ and the matter density, we compute for each mock the $Λ$CDM "shadow" model that preserves the CMB shift parameter R and produces an equivalent first acoustic peak. SO and CMB-S4 alone cannot distinguish CPL cosmology from its $Λ$CDM shadow since the posteriors in the $w_0, w_a$ plane remain broad and largely overlap. This is more pessimistic than our previous Fisher matrix expectation, showing that the Gaussian approximation underestimates the impact of the geometric degeneracy. Adding BAO and Pantheon$+$ supernovae data, yields $σ(w_0)\simeq 0.05$-$0.06$ for both experiments. However, the resulting preference for dynamical dark energy depends on the BAO data set, for instance, SDSS BAO moves the constraints close to the $Λ$CDM. Our results quantify the discriminating power of future CMB surveys in a Bayesian framework, and highlight the role of low redshift distance probes in establishing or rejecting a dynamical dark energy scenario.

3. Constraints and biases: impact of light-cone effects on power spectrum and bispectrum forecasts

Next-generation galaxy surveys will map large-scale structure over unprecedented volumes, bringing two goals within reach: constraining primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG) and detecting relativistic effects on the largest scales. On these scales, relativistic and wide-separation (light-cone) effects share the scale dependence of local-type PNG, so they must be modelled to avoid biased inferences and to recover the true uncertainties. We present joint power spectrum and bispectrum forecasts that include these effects, with a multi-tracer bispectrum covariance including the odd-parity multipoles and leading non-Gaussian terms. Neglecting light-cone effects biases local-type $f_{\rm NL}$ by $\sim2σ$ for a Euclid-like H$α$ survey and $>10σ$ for a MegaMapper-like LBG survey, mostly through lensing; wide-separation and local relativistic effects contribute $<1σ$. With these corrections included, marginalising over the evolution and magnification biases without informative priors increases the uncertainty on local-type $f_{\rm NL}$ by $\sim60\%$ for a Euclid-like power spectrum and by $\sim50\%$ for a joint power spectrum and bispectrum analysis of the even multipoles. However, the odd-parity multipoles of the single-tracer bispectrum and the multi-tracer power spectrum, which are sensitive to leading-order relativistic corrections, break most of this degeneracy. Non-local shapes are less affected; for these, a bright-faint split of the MegaMapper-like survey improves the joint constraints on the equilateral and orthogonal shapes by a factor of $\sim2$. Finally, local relativistic effects should be detectable at SNR $\sim13$, with a bright-faint split of a DESI-like BGS survey, provided the magnification bias is known to a few per cent, but only marginally, SNR $\lesssim2.5$, for the higher-redshift surveys, and not with the single-tracer bispectrum dipole.

The alignments of galaxy shapes, with one another and with the large-scale structure of the Universe, have been a topic of considerable investigation within cosmology and extragalactic astrophysics for the last 60 years. Galaxy alignments are a major systematic for weak gravitational lensing and help us learn about the processes of galaxy formation. Red early-type galaxies are known to show strong radial alignments around other galaxies; however, blue star-forming galaxies have shown little evidence of alignment. We combine SPHEREx spectrophotometry with DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys to build a full-sky catalogue of 71 million galaxies with precise redshifts. Existing simulation methods for clustering dark energy --- a large class of interesting theories which are until now not well studied --- usually rely on grid-based solvers and perturbatively derived equations, whose validity in the nonlinear regime remains unknown. In this paper, we introduce a novel fluid method based on splitting the dark energy action into two parts: a kinetic part and a potential part.
