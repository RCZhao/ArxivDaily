We revisit self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), originally proposed to resolve small-scale CDM problems and drive late-time cosmic acceleration. Using latest DESI BAO, Pantheon+/DESY5 type Ia supernova, and cosmic chronometer Hubble data, we constrain three representative SIDM scenarios including creation and annihilation. 6. Deglobalising the fit: how to search for physics beyond vacuum General Relativity in LISA catalogues
Francisco Duque, Jonathan Gair

TLDR: In multi-source gravitational-wave analyses for LISA, non-standard physical deviations can be inadvertently absorbed by nuisance signals during sequential source subtraction, a degradation quantifiable via posterior correlations to guide targeted, multi-source re-evaluations. (gemini)

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna will observe up to $\sim 10^{7}$ overlapping signals, which have to be characterised simultaneously with a global fit. This is a challenge for model comparison since rerunning the global fit for every physical model of interest, including deviations from vacuum General Relativity, is computationally demanding. An alternative is a two-step post-processing strategy: a first global fit with vacuum models only, after which a selected group of target sources is reanalysed under a different model with all other sources subtracted. We show that in this strategy a beyond-vacuum effect in a target source can be partly absorbed by the overlapping nuisance sources in the first fit, subtracted with them, and never reach the reanalysis. In the linear-signal approximation, the loss is captured by a single number, the masking ratio $\mathcal{R}\in[0,1]$. A deviation the global fit would detect at $n_σ$ is reported by post-processing at $\mathcal{R}\,n_σ$, and bounds in null tests are also reported too tight by the same factor. We show how to compute $\mathcal{R}$ from the posterior samples of the vacuum global fit, and give an algorithm that ranks the nuisance sources by how much of the effect each can absorb, so that only those most correlated with it need to be refitted with the target in a down-scaled reanalysis with a few sources. We validate our method with full Markov-chain Monte Carlo inference on a toy model of 21 overlapping chirping sinusoids, where one carries a small non-linear chirp that represents the missed physics, while the other 20 act as nuisance sources. This method can serve as a basis for tests of General Relativity in realistic LISA mock datasets and can be adapted to study the impact of systematics between different gravitational-wave sources.

The microphysics of dark matter (DM) is imprinted on cosmological structure across an enormous dynamic range. Nonlinear cosmological observables are most reliably modeled with N-body and hydrodynamic simulations. However, systematic N-body scans over DM theory space are prohibitively expensive. Machine learning emulators provide a possible solution, but are currently limited by either low resolution or only the ability to emulate specific observables. We introduce Astrolabe, a flow-matching-based generative emulator designed to be free of both constraints. Astrolabe acts directly on the 6N phase-space coordinates of N individual particles. With no voxel grid in the output, there is no voxel-resolution ceiling, and in principle any observable can be measured from inference. Astrolabe is computationally tractable via a field-particle hybrid transformer in which every particle cross-attends to multiscale field tokens, so compute and memory are linear in N. We also observe out-of-distribution generalization, with accurate emulation of dark-acoustic oscillation initial conditions. The functional forms of galactic rotation curves provide a fundamental test for models of gravity and dark matter. In this work, we conduct a rigorous statistical comparison of five distinct models against the complete sample of 175 galaxies from the SPARC database. The models under consideration are: (1) a one-parameter empirical law, physically constrained by each galaxy's measured flat disc rotation velocity ($V_{\text{flat}}$); (2) a classical two-parameter Freeman disk; (3) a three-parameter Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) dark matter halo plus baryons model; (4) a one-parameter Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) model; and (5) a two-parameter Bosma/Dark Matter Disk model. The pronounced statistical and descriptive success of this simple, physically-anchored law over more complex, established theories suggests that its functional form represents a fundamental and highly efficient organizing principle in the dynamics of disk galaxies.
