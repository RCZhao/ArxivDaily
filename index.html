1. Improving cosmological distance measurements at high redshift using Lyman-Alpha forest BAO reconstruction
Boryana Hadzhiyska, Roger de Belsunce

TLDR: Extending Zel'dovich reconstruction to the Lyman-alpha forest substantially reduces non-linear peak shifts and parameter uncertainties in simulated baryon acoustic oscillation measurements, yielding a more than 1.6-fold boost in the cosmological figure of merit for DESI-like survey geometries. (gemini)

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) measures baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) in the Lyman-$α$ (Lyman-alpha) forest and provides a robust probe of the expansion history in the matter-dominated era. Yet, non-linear structure formation broadens and shifts the BAO peak. To undo these effects, we extend standard Zel'dovich reconstruction, routinely applied to galaxy surveys, to the Lyman-alpha forest. We validate our methodology on large-volume idealized Lyman-alpha forest mocks painted on top of the \textsc{AbacusSummit} $N$-body simulations, using linear control variates to suppress sample variance and thereby reduce the error bars by a factor of up to $\approx \sqrt{50}$. In fully sampled periodic boxes, reconstruction halves the radial BAO damping scale and reduces the uncertainties on the radial ($\apar$) and transverse ($\aperp$) scaling parameters by $\approx25\%$ and $\approx15\%$, respectively. It also reduces the non-linear shift from $Δ\apar=+0.3\%$ to $+0.10\pm0.07\%$, while $\aperp$ remains unbiased. To make this approach readily applicable to observational data, we demonstrate the impact of a survey mask with a DESI-like density of $50\,\mathrm{deg}^{-2}$. For this mask, we compare two approaches to estimate the displacement field: either directly from the Lyman-alpha forest or using an external tracer with a number density of $\bar n \simeq 10^{-3}\,h^3\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, such as Lyman-break galaxies and Lyman-$α$ emitters---both key targets for DESI-II. Both approaches give comparable gains and reduce the uncertainties on $\apar$ and $\aperp$ by $\approx 27\%$ and $\approx 10\%$, respectively, with mild improvements for futuristic high-density surveys with $160\,\mathrm{deg}^{-2}$. Correspondingly, the figure of merit improves by factors of $1.66$ for fully sampled boxes, $1.65$ for a DESI-like, and $1.76$ for a high-density survey.

2. Measuring the magnetization profiles of galaxy groups and clusters
Kritti Sharma, Vikram Ravi, Elisabeth Krause

TLDR: Stacking Faraday rotation measures alongside electron density profiles from fast radio bursts and the kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect yields upper limits on halo magnetic fields while demonstrating that forthcoming Deep Synoptic Array data will achieve high-precision direct measurements in galaxy groups. (gemini)

Magnetic fields are vital in determining the physical conditions of baryons in massive halos. They are, however, notoriously difficult to observe. This impacts problems from our understanding of galaxy formation, to the observed deficit in the amplitude of thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) power spectrum that affects cosmological inferences. Although Faraday rotation measures (RMs) offer a direct probe of magnetic fields, it weights magnetic fields by the free-electron density. Several probes of free electrons, including fast radio burst (FRB) dispersion measures (DMs) and kinematic SZ (kSZ) effect, are now maturing into precision tools. We combine RM$^2$ stacks from SPICE-RACS around DESI galaxy groups and ROSAT clusters with optical depth profiles constrained jointly by the FRB DM-redshift relation and the kSZ effect, and obtain 95% upper limits on the rms line-of-sight magnetic field of $0.31$ ($>11$) in groups (clusters). Although current constraints are only limits, we forecast a first detection with larger datasets from the Deep Synoptic Array (DSA). We present the first forecast of the DSA RM grid density ($\sim365$ RMs deg$^{-2}$), which when combined with 10$^4$ FRBs would measure the field in groups to $3$-$13\%$ precision, compared to current constraints of $38$-$75\%$. Although the interpretation of current measurements remains challenging owing to depolarization along turbulent halo sightlines and the unknown coherence length, larger samples and DSA/Square Kilometer Array observations may resolve these uncertainties by measuring polarization-fraction profiles and the RM structure function using extended radio sources, respectively.

3. A Practical Guide to Sensitivity Projections for Beyond the Standard Model Physics
Francesca Acanfora, Michael J. Baker, Joaquim Iguaz Juan, Aidan Symons, Andrea Thamm

TLDR: Clarifying the distinction between goodness-of-fit and hypothesis testing in beyond-the-Standard-Model projections reveals that hypothesis tests appropriately quantify experimental reach, as illustrated through collider resonance searches and gamma-ray black hole signatures. (gemini)

Statistics is a well-established discipline, yet its application can be confusing, particularly in fields where the language of statistics is unfamiliar and when making projections, where real data is not yet available. Both of these factors are commonplace in Beyond the Standard Model physics. In this work, we clarify the distinction between goodness-of-fit tests, which assess the compatibility of an observation with a single hypothesis, and hypothesis tests, which quantify how strongly some data prefer one hypothesis over another. We emphasise that for Beyond the Standard Model sensitivity projections, hypothesis testing should be used. We give detailed examples using both Poisson and multinomially distributed data, deriving exact $p$-values and discussing Monte Carlo and asymptotic approximations. We further demonstrate that for the Asimov data set all the test statistics discussed, for both goodness-of-fit tests and hypothesis tests, can be approximated by Pearson's $χ^2$ statistic, and the difference in approaches comes down to the distribution of the test statistics. We also compare goodness-of-fit and hypothesis test $p$-values in two realistic examples. We (1) consider the reach of a muon collider on heavy spin-1 resonances and (2) provide updated projections for the potential of current and future gamma ray telescopes to probe new dark sectors via the observation of Hawking radiation from the explosion of a black hole. Finally, we discuss the CLs method, the significance and systematic errors.

4. Cosmological Constraints on Interacting Dark Matter with SPT-3G D1
Cynthia Trendafilova, Fei Ge, Ali Rida Khalife, Christian L. Reichardt

TLDR: Incorporating small-scale cosmic microwave background lensing from SPT-3G D1 alongside Planck, ACT, and DESI observations places stringent updated bounds on dark matter scattering with baryons, interactions with dark radiation, and annihilation cross-sections. (gemini)

Models of the dark sector in which the dark matter participates in nontrivial interactions can create detectable signatures in cosmological observables, providing non-terrestrial means of probing the nature of dark matter. In this work, we test dark matter-baryon scattering, dark matter-dark radiation interactions, and dark matter annihilation using measurements of the CMB and BAO. We leverage the latest SPT-3G D1 CMB measurements of the primary anisotropies, alongside the D1 MUSE lensing data, to set up-to-date bounds on these models. From the combination of Planck, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 data, we constrain the velocity-independent dark matter-baryon scattering cross-section to $σ_\mathrm{DM-b} < 4.6\times10^{-26}~\mathrm{cm^2}$, the dark matter-dark radiation momentum transfer rate to $Γ_\mathrm{0,nadm}<5.2\times10^{-8}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$, and the effective dark matter annihilation parameter, quantifying the fractional thermally averaged cross-section, to $p_\mathrm{ann}<3.7\times10^{-28}~\mathrm{cm^3/s/GeV}$, all at the 95% confidence level. When combining CMB data with the latest measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), upper bounds on extended model parameters can appear to tighten as the model space attempts to accommodate the known discrepancy between these data. Future CMB and BAO measurements will provide better clarity on reconciling this discrepancy and enhance our ability to test dark matter interactions.

8. An end-to-end framework for uncertainty-aware validation of generative models
Giada Badaracco, Sean Benevedes, Christina Reissel, Philip Harris, Gaia Grosso

TLDR: Introducing the SIGMA framework incorporates parameter uncertainties from Gaussian mixture density estimation into composite goodness-of-fit testing, preventing spurious rejections of generative models on large datasets in physical science applications. (gemini)

Density estimation with generative models is a common task in the physical sciences, for example in background estimation in particle physics. Existing machine learning techniques, however, lack methods to quantify uncertainties that can be propagated into a statistical test. That limits the validation of generative models to standard goodness-of-fit tests, in which the generative model is treated as exact (point-null hypothesis). But a model fitted to a finite training set is never exact, so if its uncertainties are neglected, any sufficiently powerful goodness-of-fit test will eventually reject it once the test set is large enough to resolve this estimation error. We propose SIGMA, an end-to-end framework combining density estimation with principled uncertainty quantification. We use Gaussian mixture models, for which uncertainties are tractable, and further construct a new goodness-of-fit test in which these uncertainties are profiled as nuisance parameters to account for the statistical error of the fit, promoting the conventional point-null test to a composite one. A rejection then signals genuine mismodeling rather than a test set large enough to resolve the error of the fit, provided the propagated uncertainties faithfully cover that error. We demonstrate the framework on a controlled two-dimensional toy model and on a collider physics benchmark modeling the QCD background process. With uncertainties included, the same model can be trusted on test sets up to an order of magnitude larger than the standard test allows. Our work thus provides a statistically grounded basis for a robust use of generative models in the physical sciences.

9. Accelerating fits in high-energy physics and beyond
Amr Elmarassy, Sebastian Schmitt, Eluned Smith

TLDR: Systematic evaluation of optimization algorithms for particle physics fits demonstrates that quasi-Newton BFGS variants combined with mixed-precision GPU computing and analytic simplifications yield tenfold speedups over standard Migrad implementations. (gemini)

Data analysis in particle physics often involves optimizing a loss function that quantifies how well a theoretical model describes the observed data, in order to estimate the parameters of the model. This is referred to as fitting. As datasets grow larger and more expensive analysis methods are used, the time required to perform this parameter estimation can be a limiting factor. This work explores techniques that can significantly accelerate the fitting process, and measures their performance on a set of example models chosen to be representative of a variety of physics fits. We compare the speed and precision of variants of several well-known optimization algorithms, namely Newton's method, BFGS, and the Migrad algorithm (commonly used in physics fits). We observe significant speedups when using some algorithms: most notably, some variants of BFGS are consistently about $10\times$ faster than Migrad across our benchmarks, while achieving the same precision. For quasi-Newtonian algorithms like BFGS and Migrad, we find that the initial value of the Hessian matrix has a large impact on the runtime. We additionally discuss methods for accelerating the evaluation of the loss function and its derivatives, using algebraic simplifications, automatic differentiation, and modern hardware features on the CPU and GPU. We find that basic algebraic simplifications of the loss function can significantly improve speed and precision. On the GPU we explore using mixed precision computation, and show that this achieves much higher throughput on most accelerators without significant precision loss. Lastly we discuss efficient parallelization strategies for both the CPU and the GPU. All of the techniques and algorithms in this paper are implemented in our new FastFit library, which was used for all of our benchmarks.

10. A gaussian-process reconstruction of gravitational-wave friction from dark standard sirens

Standard sirens provide a direct probe of the cosmic distance-redshift relation and therefore a powerful test of whether gravitational waves (GWs) propagate as predicted by general relativity on cosmological scales. In many extensions of general relativity, GW propagation is modified by an anomalous friction term, which alters the amplitude evolution and leads to a GW luminosity distance that differs from its electromagnetic counterpart. In this work, we develop a non-parametric reconstruction of this anomalous friction from dark standard sirens. We reconstruct its redshift dependence, encoded in the friction function $α_M(z)$, with a Gaussian process (GP) and derive the corresponding GW-to-EM luminosity-distance ratio $Ξ(z)$ by integration, explicitly retaining the relation between the local friction modification and its accumulated effect on the observed distance, while enforcing a monotonic and invertible GW luminosity distance. We further introduce function-space diagnostics for comparing flexible and parametric reconstructions, including prior calibration, prior-to-posterior contraction, common summaries of propagation-population degeneracies, and principal-component analysis. These tools make explicit which features are informed by the data and which are imposed by the function-space prior, providing a framework for interpretable non-parametric tests of gravitational-wave propagation.

11. Global Analysis of Nuclear Parton Distribution Functions using Markov Chain Monte Carlo
Nasim Derakhshanian

TLDR: Implementing Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling within the nCTEQ framework resolves non-Gaussian uncertainties and multimodalities in nuclear parton distribution functions, revealing that the inclusion of lighter nuclei substantially refines lead quark distributions while maintaining gluon stability. (gemini)

Global analyses of nuclear parton distribution functions (nPDFs) have traditionally used the Hessian method for uncertainty estimation, relying on a local quadratic approximation of the chi-squared function. This Gaussian approximation can be inadequate when experimental constraints are limited and parameter correlations are strong, as it cannot fully capture asymmetric uncertainties, flat directions, or multiple minima. This thesis develops a Bayesian framework for nPDF determination using Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques within the nCTEQ analysis framework. An adaptive Metropolis-Hastings algorithm enables direct sampling of the posterior distribution, revealing pronounced non-Gaussian behavior and secondary minima, particularly in the valence distributions. The analysis presents the first global single-nucleus determination of lead (Pb) PDFs based exclusively on lead data, complemented by a multi-nuclei analysis incorporating different nuclear targets through a standard analytic dependence on the mass number. Including lighter nuclei significantly constrains quark distributions and changes the extracted lead PDFs, while the gluon distribution remains comparatively stable. A comparison with Hessian-based results highlights the limitations of the Gaussian approximation in regions with strong nonlinearity or multimodality. Overall, this work establishes Bayesian MCMC inference as a flexible framework for nPDF determination, providing a more comprehensive characterization of parameter uncertainties and the underlying parameter space.

This work supports the notion that EFT-based models of alignments will be paramount in optimally extracting constraints from future shape correlations, and lays the foundation for the development of simulation-based priors for future galaxy shape samples.

Machine learning tasks in particle physics are typically expressed in terms of regression, or relaxation toward some optimal solution as defined by an objective or loss function. In this paper, we introduce a large multiplicity expansion for analyzing regression tasks, which enables significant analytic understanding. For concreteness, we study regression with energy flow polynomials (EFPs), a complete linear basis of infrared and collinear safe observables on Lorentz invariant phase space. While the expectation value of any EFP scales like a constant in the large multiplicity limit, we prove that the covariance on uniform phase space of any two EFPs is highly suppressed and scales like the inverse of the squared multiplicity. 50. Rènyi-Regularized Flow-Based Variational Inference for Bayesian Calibration

Variational inference with normalizing flows provides a flexible posterior approximation for Bayesian model calibration, but struggles when the posterior has several modes separated by regions of low probability mass. We study this behavior in a two-parameter twin experiment and regularize the variational objective. Under Gaussian observation errors and a Gaussian prior, the corresponding minimization objective is the expected Tikhonov calibration objective minus the differential entropy of the approximating distribution. However, the expected Tikhonov term favors concentration near one of the modes, while entropy maximization does not guarantee multimodality.
