3. Probing baryonic feedback using fast radio bursts and galaxy surveys
Haruki Ebina, Martin White

TLDR: Cross-correlating dispersion measures from upcoming fast radio burst catalogs with galaxy surveys via hybrid effective field theory offers a powerful framework to constrain baryonic feedback for Stage IV weak lensing, with overall precision primarily limited by electron-galaxy cross-stochasticity and higher-order electron biases. (gemini fallback)

The dispersion measure (DM) of fast radio bursts (FRB) provides a noisy measure of the free-electron column density to the burst. With next-generation FRB surveys the number of bursts will drastically increase and measurements of the baryon distribution will be possible on the Mpc scales most relevant to Stage IV weak lensing surveys. Comparing the DM auto-spectrum, DM-shear cross-spectrum and the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect, we argue that the cross-correlation of FRB catalogs with galaxy surveys offers many advantages. Hybrid Effective Field Theory (HEFT), often used in large-scale structure cosmology, is capable of modeling the clustering of electrons jointly with galaxies and matter to small scales. Using HEFT with the Padé model of baryonic feedback, we forecast that next-generation FRB surveys (combined with galaxy samples) will be able to constrain feedback at a level informative for Stage IV weak lensing surveys. In particular we should not be limited by modeling the manner in which galaxies trace the matter field. Rather constraints of the higher-order electron biases and the electron-galaxy cross-stochasticity provide the dominant limitation, and are an obvious target for improvement in order to reach percent-level constraints. The same measurements also provide a valuable means of constraining feedback in hydrodynamic simulations of galaxy formation in a way that is not tied to the modeling of particular galaxy samples.

4. Augmenting Galaxy Catalogues using Machine Learning in the FLAMINGO Simulations

TLDR: Hydrodynamic simulations are instrumental in the analysis of observations such as galaxy clustering used for the estimation of cosmological parameters. (sumy fallback)

Hydrodynamic simulations are instrumental in the analysis of observations such as galaxy clustering used for the estimation of cosmological parameters. However, for these purposes, the simulations need both a large box size and high resolution, which is computationally prohibitive. In this work we use the connection between galaxies and their host dark matter halos to populate dark-matter-only simulations, using data from the FLAMINGO simulations suite. We employ the Extremely Randomized Trees algorithm to model the connection between the mass profile, history and environment of dark matter halos and the probability distribution of the stellar mass of the galaxy contained in the halo. We use halos matched between a dark-matter-only simulation and the corresponding hydrodynamical simulation to train a model for centrals. We then apply these models to produce a new galaxy catalogue for the 2.8 Gpc FLAMINGO-10k dark-matter-only simulation as a product of this work. Our results indicate that current departures from $Λ$CDM are most significant in the expansion history, while leaving open the possibility of correlated modifications to gravitational dynamics that future Stage IV surveys can test.
