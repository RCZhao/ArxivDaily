TLDR: Analysis of Euclid observations reveals that intracluster light traces the elongated geometry of dark matter halos, transitioning from round central galaxies to elliptical outskirts that align closely with the brightest cluster galaxy. (gemini)

Intracluster light (ICL) offers a luminous route for measuring the shapes and orientations of cluster haloes. We used Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1) images to quantify ICL shapes for 205 clusters at $0.1 \leq z \leq 0.8$ selected from the eROSITA All-Sky Survey and the Dark Energy Survey. We measured the ICL properties across five bands ($I_\mathrm{E}$, $Y_\mathrm{E}$, $J_\mathrm{E}$, $H_\mathrm{E}$ and the coadded $YJH_\mathrm{E}$), and find that the shape parameters are consistent across the filters, with the $H_\mathrm{E}$ band probing the profiles to the largest cluster-centric distances ($0.1R_{200\mathrm{c}}\sim100$ kpc). The ICL ellipticity ($e=1-b/a$) distribution peaks at 0.5, consistent with halo ellipticities inferred from strong and weak lensing studies, suggesting that the ICL traces the large-scale structure of the clusters. Cluster cores, dominated by brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) light, are round ($e\sim0.2$), while the ICL becomes progressively more elongated at large radii, reaching an ellipticity of $\sim0.5$ by $0.1R_{200\mathrm{c}}$. We find that the ICL is closely aligned with the BCG (87% lie within 30$^\circ$), but shows a weaker alignment with the satellite galaxy distribution (61% within 30$^\circ$). Robust position angles could be measured using the ICL for 63% of the sample, whereas position angles could be measured for only 30% of the sample using the satellite galaxies. Stacking clusters in redshift bins allows measurements of the ICL surface brightness to $0.6$-$0.8R_{200\mathrm{c}}$ and ellipticity beyond $0.3$-$0.4R_{200\mathrm{c}}$ (300-400 kpc), but we observed a plateau in the ICL ellipticity beyond $0.1R_{200\mathrm{c}}$. These results demonstrate the capability of Euclid to characterise the ICL morphology with high statistical precision, allowing new constraints on cluster halo structure and its evolution with redshift.

TLDR: A dedicated image post-processing framework preserves extended diffuse emission in Euclid observations, enabling highly accurate measurements of intracluster light out to redshift 0.8 down to extremely low surface brightness levels. (gemini)

The Euclid survey provides images with extraordinary potential for studies of the low-surface-brightness Universe, including intracluster light (ICL). However, to realise this promise, the data must be carefully treated to minimise background variations while preserving the signal from diffuse structures. In this work, we describe the post-processing and measurement procedures used for studies of ICL with Euclid. We demonstrate that we are able to substantially reduce background variations without resorting to a small-scale background subtraction. By injecting simulated clusters into the real images, we show that our approach preserves the faint outskirts of extended sources. On scales of several arcminutes, we can reliably detect surface-brightness levels around 27.5 mag arcsec$^{-2}$ in $Y_\textrm{E}$, $J_\textrm{E}$, $H_\textrm{E}$, and 29 mag arcsec$^{-2}$ in $I_\textrm{E}$. As a result, we can obtain accurate measurements of the ICL in individual clusters from the local Universe out to z~0.8 with uncertainties on the order of 10% in surface brightness, 10 degrees in position angle, and 0.1 in ellipticity. Our ability to measure the diffuse light with Euclid is thus set to meet expectations and revolutionise our understanding of the ICL.

TLDR: Stacked radial color profiles from Euclid demonstrate that intracluster light is systematically bluer and more metal-poor than the central galaxy, indicating that diffuse cluster stars originate from the tidal stripping of satellite galaxy outskirts. (gemini)

Colour provides key information to constrain the stellar population of the intracluster light (ICL), the diffuse light within galaxy clusters. We derive radial colour profiles ($Y_\mathrm{E}-H_\mathrm{E}$, $J_\mathrm{E}-H_\mathrm{E}$, $I_\mathrm{E}-H_\mathrm{E}$) of the brightest central galaxy (BCG) and ICL for 168 galaxy clusters from the Dark Energy Survey redMaPPer catalogue within the redshift range $0.1 10^{14.05}M_\odot/h$, within the Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). By stacking the light profiles measured using elliptical annuli on the BCG and ICL images, we reliably measure the ICL colour out to approximately 300 kpc, over which we find a colour gradient, with the ICL becoming bluer than the BCG out to approximately 150 kpc. Using stellar population synthesis (SPS) models, we find that near-infrared (NIR) colours are sensitive to metallicity, but not age. Thus, we measure a metallicity profile in the BCG and ICL that extends out to 300 kpc for a statistical sample for the first time. While the colours, and the detection of a colour and metallicity gradient, are robust, we note that our metallicity measurement is dependent upon our choice of both SPS model and colour. We find that the metallicity of the BCG is super-solar [$\log_{10} (Z/Z_\odot) > 0.1$], while the ICL is metal-poor [$\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)\approx -0.5$], placing the first statistical constraint on the metallicity of the ICL out to large radii. These results support our current understanding of BCG and ICL formation: BCGs form predominantly through mergers with massive galaxies or the cores of satellite galaxies, while the ICL grows via the tidal stripping of lower-metallicity stars from the outskirts of massive satellite galaxies.
