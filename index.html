1. The connection between massive galaxies and their dark matter haloes from $z=0.25$ to $z \sim 4$ from the combined COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields
Natalia Stylianou, Matt J. Jarvis, Rohan G. Varadaraj, Peter W. Hatfield, Joel Hamlett, Catherine L. Hale, Tariq Yasin

TLDR: Large-scale galaxy clustering measurements across $0.25 < z < 4$ reveal that star formation efficiency peaks within $\sim 10^{12} M_\odot$ dark matter haloes at $z \sim 1$, below which stellar growth in massive central galaxies plateaus despite ongoing hierarchical halo assembly, indicating late-time suppression driven by active galactic nuclei feedback. (gemini fallback)

Understanding how galaxies populate and grow within dark matter haloes is essential for linking the observed evolution of galaxies to the hierarchical growth of structure. We present a galaxy clustering analysis using optical and near-infrared data from the UltraVISTA and VIDEO surveys covering $9.3$ deg$^2$ across the COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields, $\sim 5\times$ larger than previous comparable studies, providing sufficient volume to determine the halo occupation of the most massive galaxies to $z\sim 4$. We measure the angular two-point correlation function for stellar-mass-selected galaxies over $0.25<z<4$ and by combining the three fields, we reduce the cosmic variance by a factor of $\sim2$ compared to the individual fields. The stellar-to-halo mass ratio peaks at a halo mass of $M_{h}\approx10^{12} {\rm M}_{\odot}$ and is a maximum at $z\sim 1$. For the most massive galaxies, the peak halo mass ($M_{h}^{\rm peak}$) increases from $z=1\rightarrow 0$, while the peak stellar mass in such haloes remains approximately constant, indicating that at $z<1$ massive galaxy formation becomes less efficient. We also find a steady increase in the satellite fraction since $z\sim 1$, from which we infer that massive halos continue to grow hierarchically while their central galaxies experience suppressed stellar-mass growth. We suggest this is due to the central massive galaxies having their growth controlled by feedback from active galactic nuclei feedback, likely from low-power stochastic radio emission.

2. Cosmology with strong gravitational lenses
Tommaso Treu

TLDR: Serving as a purely gravitational probe of expansion dynamics and non-luminous mass, strong lensing delivers crucial independent constraints on the Hubble constant, dark energy, and dark matter substructure, establishing an observational framework set to expand dramatically with upcoming wide-field surveys. (gemini fallback)

Three of the most pressing open questions in cosmology -- the nature of dark energy and dark matter, and the growing tension in the value of the Hubble constant H$_0$ -- call for cosmological probes that are independent of, and complementary to, traditional methods. Strong gravitational lensing offers exactly that: a purely gravitational probe of the universe's expansion history and matter distribution, sensitive to dark matter's presence regardless of whether it interacts with light. Time-delay lenses and multiplane lenses can measure H$_0$ and the dark energy equation of state directly, while the distortion of lensed images can reveal the abundance and internal structure of dark matter subhalos. After a brief theoretical and historical introduction, I review recent breakthroughs enabled by JWST and by advances in lens modeling and stellar dynamics. Examples include the recent measurement of H$_0$ with 3\% precision in flat $Λ$ cold dark matter cosmology, and a tentative detection of dark matter self-interaction. Strong lensing is entering a pivotal era: wide-field surveys with Euclid, Rubin, and Roman, and radio surveys with the Square Kilometer Array, are poised to increase the number of known strong lenses by orders of magnitude, positioning strong lensing to play a central role in answering these open questions in the decade ahead.

3. GroArnold: A Framework for Constructing Action-based Models for Triaxial Galaxies and Dark Matter Halos
Jian-Yu Gu, Xiao-Bo Dong, Shi-Yan Zhong, Run-Yu Meng

TLDR: GroArnold provides an end-to-end computational framework that couples $N$-body simulations with extended action-estimation techniques to construct dynamically stable, analytical action-based distribution functions for axisymmetric and triaxial galactic systems. (gemini fallback)

Action-based distribution functions $f(\mathbf{J})$ provide a powerful description of stellar systems. Consequently, the construction of physically accurate and dynamically stable $f(\mathbf{J})$ for galactic components has emerged as a key challenge in the field. In this study, we present GroArnold, a computational framework designed to construct analytical $f(\mathbf{J})$ expressions for axisymmetric and triaxial galaxies and dark matter halos. The framework integrates modules for $N$-body initial condition generation, $N$-body simulation, angle--action variable estimation, and distribution-function fitting into a unified workflow. It supports a wide range of density profiles and kinematic properties, and is uniquely designed to generate stable $N$-body models for user-specified galactic systems, with the aim of obtaining an $f(\mathbf{J})$ that approximates a dynamically stable and physically consistent state. To this end, we extend the triaxial Stäckel Fudge method of the TACT code, implemented in our Nbody-TACT module, to enable action estimation directly from $N$-body gravitational potentials. GroArnold provides a robust and flexible tool for deriving numerically empirical, explicitly analytical distribution functions for user-specified galaxies or halos, thereby facilitating a wide range of practical $f(\mathbf{J})$-based modeling applications. In addition, it serves as a powerful tool for investigating the dynamics of realistic triaxial galaxies and halos beyond the scope of traditional spherical or axisymmetric models. We assessed the accuracy of the output $f(\mathbf{J})$ by comparing its predicted spatial and velocity distributions with those of the input $N$-body snapshot, and confirmed that it provides a reasonably accurate representation of the input. The code is modular, publicly available, and designed to support future extensions and adaptations.

4. Low-cost neural summaries for cosmological inference: an ensemble of convolutional neural networks trained on approximate simulations
Iñigo Sáez-Casares, Davide Bianchi, Matteo Calabrese, Luigi Guzzo

TLDR: Ensembles of convolutional neural networks trained on inexpensive, low-resolution simulations yield highly transferable summary statistics that constrain cosmological parameters from high-resolution data and dark matter halo catalogs more effectively than traditional power spectrum analyses. (gemini fallback)

Machine learning can build near-optimal summary statistics for extracting cosmological information from the large-scale structure of the Universe. Training them, however, requires large numbers of costly simulations. We aim to build a neural summary trained on fast, approximate low-resolution simulations that remains informative when applied to higher-resolution ones, reducing the cost of the training set. We train an ensemble of convolutional neural networks with different architectures to compress the matter overdensity fields of low-resolution particle-mesh simulations. The outputs of a subset of networks are concatenated into a summary statistic, which we use to constrain $Ω_{\rm m}$ and $σ_8$ from high-resolution particle-mesh simulations, each about a thousand times more costly than their low-resolution counterparts. Theoretical predictions are provided by a Gaussian process emulator built from the high-resolution simulations. We test three strategies for selecting the smallest informative subset of networks. Constraints are compared with those from a reference power spectrum analysis. We also consider dark matter halo catalogues from independent high-resolution $N$-body simulations. The neural summary extracts information beyond the binned power spectrum. Using multiple networks is essential: a single network can be less informative than the power spectrum. A greedy search based on posterior volume provides the tightest constraints with the fewest networks. The neural summary also outperforms the power spectrum for dark matter haloes, at different number densities and redshifts, despite being trained on low-resolution matter fields at $z=0$. This work demonstrates that an ensemble of networks trained on approximate simulations yields a summary statistic that remains informative on higher-fidelity data, including dark matter haloes. Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) stand as prime targets for future space-borne gravitational wave (GW) detectors, yet their precise parameter estimation is severely hindered by waveform complexity and high-dimensional parameter spaces. In this work, we propose a deep learning framework capable of recovering the seven intrinsic EMRI parameters without relying on detailed priors for extrinsic parameters. Our approach first employs a U-Net to extract robust signal features from noisy time-frequency data. These features are then fed into a multi-channel convolutional neural network (CNN) equipped with a dual attention mechanism, which decouples the time and frequency dimensions to jointly model long-range temporal evolution and persistent spectral structures, thereby enhancing sensitivity to EMRI time-frequency ridges in noisy data. The thermal properties of the \(ρ\) meson are revisited within the soft-wall AdS/QCD framework. The dilaton background is calibrated against lattice QCD screening-mass data through a neural-network-assisted inverse procedure. Using this calibrated background, we compute the temporal vector spectral function and the lowest vector quasi-normal modes. The pole mass \(m_{\mathrm{pole}}(T)\) and thermal width \(Γ(T)\) are then extracted from the corresponding complex quasi-normal mode frequencies. This form modifies the usual relation between the screening and pole masses. These results may provide a useful reference for effective studies of the hot hadronic medium.

49. Drift parameter estimation for interacting particle systems with singular repulsion

We study the estimation of the interaction parameter in singular interacting particle systems from continuous and discrete observations over a fixed time horizon. The singularity of the interaction drift prevents the direct application of standard likelihood and contrast-based methods, which rely on regularity assumptions that fail in this setting. Our approach exploits an exact reduction of the particle dynamics to a one-dimensional stochastic differential equation for a quadratic empirical moment. By the symmetry of the interaction kernel, the singular terms cancel, yielding an explicit estimator in which the unknown parameter enters linearly. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality as the number of particles, \(N\), tends to infinity. Letting \(\varepsilon_N\) denote the microscopic noise scaling of the system, we obtain asymptotic normality at the rate \(\sqrt{N/\varepsilon_N}\) for strictly positive interaction kernels, under an additional condition on the limiting empirical information. We further extend these results to degenerate interaction kernels, achieving consistency and asymptotic normality at rate \(N\) under suitable structural assumptions and the canonical scaling \(\varepsilon_N=N^{-1}\). In both regimes, we provide conditions on the observation mesh ensuring that the statistical limits remain valid under discrete sampling, in high frequency. Finally, we illustrate the applicability of our methodology through Dyson Brownian motion, the Dyson Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process, and Wishart--Laguerre particle systems, alongside a numerical investigation of the estimators' finite-sample performance.

Preference optimization ranks final answers and trajectory RL maximizes aggregate returns, yet neither credits the local change that turns a flawed reasoning state into a better one. We introduce Lifted State Policy Optimization (LSPO), which establishes this finite, cost-adjusted transition as the primary unit of learning. Each textual answer is augmented with a learned continuous coordinate, forming a lifted state that an energy function evaluates and orders.
