1. Galaxy bias of radio AGN and star-forming galaxies from MIGHTEE and CMB lensing
Joel Hamlett, Catherine L. Hale, Matt J. Jarvis, David Alonso, S. Lyla Jung, Natalia Stylianou, Rohan G. Varadaraj

TLDR: Cross-correlating MIGHTEE radio galaxy positions with Atacama Cosmology Telescope CMB lensing measurements constrains the redshift evolution of cosmic galaxy bias and disentangles the clustering properties of active galactic nuclei from star-forming galaxies. (gemini)

Radio galaxies play a significant role in galaxy evolution through feedback mechanisms, making it important to characterize the environments in which they reside. We measure the cross-correlation between the positions of radio sources in the MIGHTEE survey and cosmic microwave background lensing measurements from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope over $20\ {\rm deg}^2$ to flux densities $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}>40\ {\rm μJy}$, detecting the signal at $\sim4σ$ significance. By combining this with auto-correlation measurements of low- and high-redshift radio samples, we constrain the redshift evolution of the galaxy bias parameter, $b(z)$. Fitting different models for the redshift evolution of the bias, we find that both a model inversely proportional to the linear growth factor, $b(z) = (1.63^{+0.19}_{-0.22})/D(z)$, and a model where bias grows linearly with redshift, $b(z) = (0.49^{+0.44}_{-0.32}) + (1.62^{+0.37}_{-0.51})z$, provide comparably good fits to the data, with the current measurements unable to distinguish between them. Using a radio luminosity function, we estimate the fraction of sources that are active galactic nuclei (AGNs) or star-forming galaxies (SFGs) as a function of redshift, and model the individual contributions of AGNs and SFGs to the total bias. This approach allows us to measure the bias of AGNs and SFGs without individual source classifications. Though no single model is favoured significantly, the best-fitting model gives $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (2.60^{+0.65}_{-0.91})/D(z)$ compared to $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.52^{+0.47}_{-0.34})/D(z)$. Incorporating SFG bias measurements from previous work as a prior tightens these constraints to $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (1.96^{+0.37}_{-0.40})/D(z)$ and $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.90^{+0.06}_{-0.06})/D(z)$.

2. Can coupled thawing quintessence reproduce DESI DR2? A test with two potentials
Jhananii Yuvaraj Babu, Luca Amendola

TLDR: Coupled quintessence with thawing scalar potentials can reproduce the apparent dark energy phantom crossing favored by DESI DR2 observations, achieving substantial statistical improvements over standard Lambda-CDM cosmology. (gemini)

DESI DR2 favours dark energy whose equation of state evolves crossing $w=-1$ in the past. Coupled quintessence can produce such an apparent phantom crossing without a phantom field, since a coupled dark sector analysed under the assumption of separate conservation yields an effective equation of state below $-1$. We test whether this mechanism can reproduce the DESI result, using two thawing potentials, a cosine and a double-exponential hilltop, confronted with DESI DR2 BAO, Planck PR4 and three supernova compilations. Both models improve on $Λ$CDM by $Δχ^2 = 5$--$15$, recovering $64$--$92\%$ of the improvement achieved by $w_0w_a$CDM. They track the $w_0w_a$CDM equation of state at $z\lesssim0.4$ but cross the phantom divide later, near $z\simeq1$ rather than $z\simeq0.4$--$0.5$. The coupling is bounded at $|β|<0.073$ ($95\%$), and the two potentials agree on it to within $0.15σ$. They differ instead in the equation of state they can reach: the cosine comes closer to the evolution preferred by DESI and is weakly preferred by the fit. The coupling posterior is bimodal, with modes at $β\simeq\pm0.05$; only the negative mode produces the crossing, and it is the higher of the two in every analysis. In conclusion, the data favour the coupled quintessence phantom crossing but do not yet require it.

3. Topological Defects in Cosmic Shear
Jiachen Bai, Benjamin Giblin, Andy Taylor

TLDR: Analyzing the phase-based topological defects of weak lensing shear orientation fields provides complementary cosmological information that substantially tightens constraints on matter density and amplitude of fluctuations relative to standard two-point statistics. (gemini)

In this work, we present a novel approach for extracting cosmological information from cosmic shear maps based on topological defects in the shear orientation field. These defects are defined purely by the phase structure of the shear field and are therefore complementary to conventional weak lensing statistics that primarily probe amplitude information. We characterise their statistical properties, including number densities, spatial clustering, and the convergence distribution at defect locations, and demonstrate with Fisher analyses under idealised and realistic survey conditions that topological defect statistics provide a valuable and complementary source of cosmological information. 5. ENFR: Evidence Networks with Flow-Matched References for Amortized Simulation-Based Model Inference
Kai Lehman, Jameson Dong, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro, Niall Jeffrey, Benjamin D. Wandelt, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro, Sven Krippendorf, Jochen Weller

TLDR: A machine learning framework combining flow-matched reference distributions with evidence networks enables fast, amortized, and accurate computation of absolute Bayesian model evidence for high-dimensional goodness-of-fit and model comparison tasks. (gemini)

The evidence, $\mathcal{Z} = p(x | \mathcal{M})$, of a data-generating model $\mathcal{M}$ is a powerful tool in Bayesian frameworks: it enables model inference, model misspecification tests, and goodness-of-fit assessment as an alternative to standard discrepancy statistics, e.g. $χ^2$. While versatile, the evidence is notoriously difficult to compute, even when the likelihood is available. In both likelihood-based and simulation-based inference, estimating $\mathcal{Z}$ provides an immediate solution to high-dimensional goodness-of-fit or model misspecification tests: using $\mathcal{Z}(x)$ as the test statistic induces a minimum-volume level-$α$ test. Such tests are essential to assess model fidelity, which is necessary to trust simulation-based parameter inference. Existing learned approaches largely target Bayes factors, which only measure evidence ratios. Instead, we provide accurate, fast, and amortized estimates of the absolute evidence. We present ENFR: Evidence Networks with Flow-matched References. Building on the Evidence Network framework of Jeffrey & Wandelt (2024), the proposed method first learns Bayes factors with respect to a flow-matched reference, from which the absolute evidence is recovered. We show improvement in estimation of $\mathcal{Z}$ over the flow-based baseline in all applications considered and the best performance of all density estimation methods considered in our benchmarks. We furthermore introduce and provide the proof for an architecture that jointly learns the individual evidences of a set of models $\{\mathcal{M}_i\}$. We demonstrate the performance and scaling behavior of these approaches on data from a variety of scientific applications.

6. Primordial Power Spectrum from the Lensed CMB Temperature Spectrum using Iterative Delensing and Fourier Denoising

There are many proposed models, from the simpler power law form to various well motivated physical models that predict features in the PPS beyond the power law. Given the large parameter space of possible models, it is necessary to have model independent searches for such features. Weak gravitational lensing alters the CMB temperature power spectrum and can bias free form reconstructions of the primordial power spectrum (PPS) if not accounted for precisely, such as when using estimators like the Richardson-Lucy (RL) deconvolution algorithm. We provide a detailed description of these results and recommend its utility for future model independent searches for hints of novel physics in the form of deviations from a power law PPS. This can further assist in precise and accurate testing of inflation models, in conjunction with joint cosmological parameter estimation.

7. Possible detection of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations imprinted in the HI sources from the FASHI catalog
Felipe Avila, Camila Franco, Armando Bernui

TLDR: Large-scale angular clustering measurements of local neutral hydrogen from the FAST All Sky HI Survey reveal a tentative transverse baryon acoustic oscillation feature near fifty degrees that is consistent with projection effects at low redshift. (gemini)

Motivated by curiosity as to whether the observed Universe at low redshifts is well described by the concordance cosmological model, we investigate the large scale angular clustering of extragalactic neutral hydrogen HI sources in the local Universe. Using the second data release of the FAST All Sky HI Survey (FASHI), we analyze a sample of 142,204 HI sources up to $z<0.09$, with a median redshift of $\bar{z} = 0.034$, covering $\sim 40\%$ of the sky. We compute the 2-point angular correlation function, $w(θ)$, and estimate its covariance using an analytical Gaussian model. We report the possible detection of a broad angular Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) signal, peaking near $50^\circ$. A comparison between a 5th-order polynomial fit and a quadratic baseline yields $Δχ^2 = 9.06$ for $Δ\mathrm{dof} = 3$ (difference of 3 degrees of freedom), corresponding to a nominal $1.9\,σ$ significance for a non-monotonic feature in $w(θ)$ relative to the monotonic quadratic baseline, although this result alone does not establish a BAO detection. Our analyses show that, due to the projection effect, the expected transverse BAO scale shifts from $64^\circ$ in an infinitesimally thin redshift bin to $52^\circ$ in a bin of thickness $Δz = 0.09$, like the data we study, a value close to where our detection peaks, i.e., $\sim 50^\circ$. Furthermore, comparisons with an ensemble of lognormal mocks show that the observed broad bump is consistent with statistical fluctuations at the $1σ$ level, meaning that current uncertainties prevent us from unequivocally establishing it as a cosmological signal. Comprehensively, our study establishes a valuable observational baseline for wide-field HI surveys, showing the potential to trace primordial acoustic features even in the low redshift regime, where the transverse BAO signal is dominated by projection effects.
