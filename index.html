1. Sound-Horizon-Agnostic Forecast for Constraints on $H_0$ and $Σm_ν$ from BAO, CMB Lensing, Supernovae and LSST-like Galaxy Lensing
Seyed Hamidreza Mirpoorian, Helena García Escudero, Levon Pogosian, Amy Wayland, David Alonso

TLDR: Combining future galaxy and CMB lensing with baryon acoustic oscillations and supernovae under a sound-horizon-agnostic framework can constrain the Hubble constant and neutrino masses without early-Universe calibrations, though its accuracy remains sensitive to specific early-time cosmological physics. (gemini)

We present a sound-horizon-agnostic forecast for constraints on the Hubble constant $H_0$ and the sum of the neutrino masses $Σm_ν$ from a combination of LSST-like galaxy weak lensing, Simons-Observatory-like CMB lensing, the completed DESI BAO program, and expanded supernova samples. Treating the sound horizon at baryon drag $r_d$ as a free parameter, the method provides a consistency test of $Λ$CDM without relying on the standard early-Universe calibration of $r_d$. The analysis incorporates realistic galaxy weak-lensing systematics, explicitly marginalizing over photometric-redshift calibration, multiplicative shear biases, intrinsic alignments, and baryonic feedback, and extends the CMB-lensing analysis to $L_{\max}=5000$. For fixed $Σm_ν$, we forecast uncertainties of $σ(H_0)\simeq 1.5$ km/s/Mpc with our least informative prior set, $σ(H_0)\simeq 1.3$ km/s/Mpc with more restrictive priors, and $σ(H_0)\simeq 0.7$ km/s/Mpc with an additional informative prior on the amplitude of primordial fluctuations $A_s$. Allowing $Σm_ν$ to vary weakens the $H_0$ constraints, while still yielding a forecast 95% CL upper limit of about 0.4 eV on $Σm_ν$. We also clarify the scope of the $r_d$-agnostic approach through validation tests using mock data from modified recombination and early dark energy models. The method recovers unbiased constraints on $H_0$ for the former but not the latter, highlighting its dependence on assumptions about early-Universe physics.

2. Robust paths to primordial $B$ modes in the presence of polarised point source contamination
Arianna Rizzieri, Giorgia Zagatti, David Alonso

TLDR: Masking bright extragalactic radio sources above approximately 0.1 Jy while modeling residual point-source emissions effectively prevents foreground biases in ground-based cosmic microwave background polarization measurements without compromising cosmological parameter precision. (gemini)

A detection of primordial $B$ modes in the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) polarisation relies on a robust control and removal of contamination from astrophysical polarised foreground emission. We examine how much polarised extragalactic point sources may contaminate the cosmological signal in ground-based CMB experiments, propagating their impact through component separation to the final parameter constraints. We discuss masking and modelling strategies to ensure that the resulting constraints remain both accurate and precise under this source of contamination. We produce synthetic data containing polarised emission from extragalactic radio sources (ERSs) and dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) following state-of-the-art models for both samples. We find that, while the DSFG contribution is likely too small to cause any significant bias to the cosmological or foreground parameters, the ERS contribution instead, if left unmasked, leads to a bias in the parameters describing the Galactic synchrotron and lensing $B$-mode components. For experiments targeting noise levels of order $σ_P\simeq 1\,μK\,{\rm arcmin}$, we find that masking all ERSs with fluxes above $\sim$0.1 Jy, coupled with a relatively simple model to marginalise over residual point-source contamination, achieves a suitable balance that allows for the robust recovery of unbiased cosmological and foreground parameters at a negligible cost in parameter uncertainties.

(gemini)

Cosmological cross-correlation analyses are commonly based on two-point statistics, which are optimal for Gaussian random fields but do not capture all relevant information in the presence of non-Gaussianity, which is ubiquitous in late-time cosmic fields. This motivates investigations of higher order statistics, and one method is to use machine learning to construct a correlation-optimized tracer field that can utilize cross-correlation information beyond two-point statistics. Using a tracer field as input and a paired target field as part of the cost function, we test whether a U-Net can learn a transformed tracer field that maximally correlates with the target field. We train a 1D U-Net on paired simulated fields and study controlled cases based on Gaussian and log-normal random fields. This indicates that the network is sensitive to information beyond the power spectrum. These controlled 1D experiments show that U-Net-based tracer transformation can effectively extract correlation-relevant information beyond the power spectrum, providing a promising framework for enhancing cosmological cross-correlation analyses.

6. Density-split analyses of the pairwise kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect
Finn A. Roper, Aritra K. Gon, Yan-Chuan Cai, John A. Peacock

TLDR: Decomposing galaxy samples by local environmental density isolates opposing infalling and outflowing velocity fields, mitigating pairwise cancellation in the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect and dramatically boosting constraints on cosmological parameters such as $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}$. (gemini)

We consider the dependence of the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich (kSZ) effect on the environments of foreground cosmological structures. Standard pairwise kSZ estimators exploit the fact that galaxies below separations of $\sim 200\,h^{-1}{\rm M}_\odot$ fall towards each other on average, yielding a negative mean pairwise kSZ signal. However, this signal is the residual from a near-cancellation between outflows from underdense regions and infall towards overdensities giving a large variance in the pairwise velocity distribution and hence a low signal-to-noise ratio for the mean kSZ signal. This effect is especially marked at the largest scales, where the pairwise velocity distribution becomes more symmetric about zero. We show that splitting galaxies according to their local overdensity allows the convergence and divergence flows to be separated, yielding pairwise kSZ signals of opposing sign, with a reduction in variance for each subset. We use simulations to perform a detailed analysis of this phenomenon, comparing the cosmological information content in the global pairwise kSZ signal with our new density-split pairwise kSZ estimator. We consider galaxy catalogues comparable to datasets from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, together with cosmic microwave background instrumental noise characteristic of recent and planned ground-based experiments. We predict that the density-split pairwise kSZ will increase the significance of detection by only a modest factor; nevertheless, we expect improvements by factors of respectively 11 and 4 in constraining $σ_8$ and $Ω_{\rm m}$ when using the combination of Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument and Simons Observatory Large Aperture Telescope surveys.
