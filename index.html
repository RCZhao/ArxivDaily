2. Modified recombination or dynamical dark energy (or both)?
Alexander Reeves, Gabriel P. Lynch, Andrina Nicola, Jens Chluba, Alexandre Refregier

TLDR: DDE has the lowest Akaike information criterion of the models we consider for every data combination, and adding recombination freedom to it raises the AIC by $5.1$--$11.7$ and is disfavoured by the Bayesian evidence. (sumy fallback)

Several recent analyses report a hint of modified recombination when the latest cosmic microwave background (CMB) and baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data are jointly analysed. We examine whether this preference persists when allowing for departures in the late-time expansion history of the Universe. We fit a seven-parameter reconstruction of the free-electron fraction and a $w_0w_a$ dark-energy model, separately and jointly, to DESI DR2 BAO, a $5\times2$pt combination of galaxy clustering and CMB lensing, and three CMB likelihoods: P-ACT, Planck PR4 and Planck 2018. With a cosmological constant, our baseline data combination favours early recombination near last scattering, agreeing with previous analyses. Allowing for dynamical dark energy (DDE) removes this preference, while the constraints on $w_0$ and $w_a$ change little when the recombination history is marginalised over. DDE has the lowest Akaike information criterion of the models we consider for every data combination, and adding recombination freedom to it raises the AIC by $5.1$--$11.7$ and is disfavoured by the Bayesian evidence. We interpret these results in terms of CMB predictions for the BAO distances: earlier recombination and DDE both shift these predictions towards the measurements, but only DDE also changes the redshift dependence of the late-time expansion, which anisotropic BAO measurements constrain. We support these conclusions using noiseless mock data, finding that fitting modified recombination to a mock Universe generated with DDE reproduces the apparent preference for modified recombination, whereas DDE cannot similarly reproduce the signatures of modified recombination. The preference for modified recombination found with a cosmological constant therefore reflects the discrepancy between the CMB and BAO, rather than independent evidence for departures from standard recombination physics.

3. Lensing ratios as a geometric probe of dark energy in the LSST and Simons Observatory era

Ratios of galaxy-CMB and galaxy-galaxy lensing around the same foreground galaxies probe relative distances extending to recombination without a standard candle or ruler. For sufficiently narrow foreground redshift distributions, and with intrinsic alignments and lens magnification controlled, these lensing ratios (LRs) become geometrical. Here we characterize the geometrical limit and develop an end-to-end optimized framework for the probe with LSST Y10 and Simons Observatory uncertainties. In our forecast for LSST Y10, the individual ratios are measured to a median precision of 1.0%, with a total signal-to-noise ratio of 360. 4. Enhanced spatial resolution and performance for N-body simulation of self-interacting dark matter
Moritz S. Fischer

TLDR: As a consequence, a typical SIDM simulation of isotropic scattering requires only a few percent of its total computational cost for the self-interactions. (sumy fallback)

Studying the implications of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) on astrophysical objects often requires simulations. N-body simulations are a standard tool but face a few challenges. One is resolving the mean free path in gravothermally collapsing halos. This is necessary to obtain accurate results and is achieved in state-of-the-art simulations by increasing the number of resolution elements. I aim to formulate a numerical scheme that gives an enhanced spatial resolution compared to those methods and allows increasing the resolution without changing the number of resolution elements. The novel scheme that I present is formulated by discarding the classic approach of using kernel functions and computing interaction probabilities. Instead, pairs of particles always interact if their distance is below a specific threshold. Importantly, this distance threshold depends on their relative velocity and the size of the time step. This new formulation no longer requires some of the computations in state-of-the-art schemes and thus helps to increase the performance. Through a variety of test problems with known solutions, I study the accuracy of the new scheme and demonstrate its advantages over state-of-the-art SIDM formulations. Notably, the scheme reduced the computational costs, making the SIDM computations typically much cheaper than gravity. As a consequence, a typical SIDM simulation of isotropic scattering requires only a few percent of its total computational cost for the self-interactions. In conclusion, the new scheme presents an improvement over previous schemes and helps to address current challenges. Besides the enhanced performance, it allows us to achieve a higher accuracy when simulating gravothermally collapsing SIDM halos.

We use symbolic regression to diagnose the parameter dependence of a black-box multi-fidelity emulator of the Lyman-alpha forest 1D flux power spectrum. Symbolic regression is appealing in its ability to reduce complex parameter spaces, thereby reducing compute time and offering potential interpretability in parameter inter-dependencies. Starting from a pretrained Gaussian-process (GP) emulator that connects low- and high-resolution PRIYA simulations, we search for explicit equations describing the dependence of the power spectrum on resolution and each cosmological and astrophysical parameter. Our methods present potential mitigation strategies for failure modes of PySR, enabling downstream Fisher forecasts in some of these cases. Further, we document limitations potentially relevant to future work bridging symbolic regression with emulators. Our results demonstrate symbolic regression as an emulator diagnostic tool, with particular utility for low-dimensional or weakly coupled parameter dependencies.
