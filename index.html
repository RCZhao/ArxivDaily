1. Odd Loops: What Structures Can Produce a Parity-Violating Inflationary Trispectrum?
William Ortolá Leonard, Zachary Slepian

TLDR: Perturbative analysis of inflationary non-Gaussianity reveals that parity-violating trispectra can arise from only a single third-order Wick contraction configuration with three independent wavevectors, whereas second-order isotropic contractions cannot generate any parity-violating contributions. (gemini)

Non-Gaussian perturbations generated during inflation can often be described perturbatively via integrals of kernels acting on Gaussian fields. In this work, we investigate, in a model-independent manner, whether these kernels can produce parity-violating (PV) contributions to the trispectrum, and which Wick's theorem contraction configurations of the Gaussian fields lead to them. In detail, we evaluate all possible contraction configurations arising from the third- and second-order density perturbations. We find that, for the third-order perturbations, only one configuration can support PV contributions, as it retains three independent wave-vectors. In contrast, none of the four second-order perturbation contraction configurations can produce a PV contribution under statistical isotropy. These results offer a first step toward deriving kernels from inflationary models capable of generating a PV trispectrum. Such kernels could then be used to perform PV searches with current and upcoming galaxy redshift surveys such as DESI, Roman, SPHEREx, and Euclid, and photometric efforts such as LSST. Our results also offer a guide for exploration of PV with CMB data-sets such as Planck.

2. Lessons from double-plane lenses for galaxy profiles, the Hubble tension, and the mass-sheet degeneracy
Daniel P. Johnson, Martin Millon, Pierre Fleury, Giacomo Queirolo, Tian Li, Wolfgang J. R. Enzi, Daniel J. Ballard, Duncan Bowden, Thomas E. Collett, Daniel Gilman

TLDR: Utilizing the relative expansion history in double-plane gravitational lens systems can break the mass-sheet degeneracy without requiring stellar kinematics or time delays, potentially boosting single-lens Hubble constant precision by up to 50% while placing tight constraints on deflector mass sheets. (gemini)

Double-plane lenses, like single-plane lenses, are subject to the mass-sheet degeneracy, a fundamental limit to our knowledge of the deflector mass profiles from lensing. However, with a prior on the relative expansion history, unlike in the single-plane case, this degeneracy can be constrained with the modelling of lens images, without the need for time delays, velocity dispersion measurements or a prior on the Hubble constant $H_0$. We show that double-plane lenses are sensitive to a specific combination of the mass-sheet components of the two lens planes. Using literature values of the cosmological scaling factor from the double-plane lenses J0946 and AGEL0353, a measurement from the point-source images of J1721 (the ``Einstein zig-zag'' lens), and a reference value of $Ω_\mathrm{m}$, we show the constraints that can be placed on the mass-sheet component of each of these systems. We show that the additional information brought by images of a second source can improve measurements of $H_0$ from individual time-delay lenses by up to $\sim50\%$, depending on certain key assumptions. Using forecasted Euclid and LSST double-plane lens samples, we show that these samples will enable strong constraints on the population of mass sheets in lens galaxies. Such population constraints will have implications for a range of strong-lensing science applications, in particular in constraining $H_0$, dark energy, and the dark matter profiles of galaxies.

3. New optimisations for exact, scalable 2D Gaussian process inference, with applications to astronomical time-series
Mark Fortune, Neale P. Gibson, Benjamin Pope

TLDR: A new JAX-based framework leverages Kronecker product algebra to scale exact two-dimensional Gaussian process regression to millions of datapoints, mitigating unmodeled correlated noise in multi-wavelength observations such as exoplanet transit spectroscopy. (gemini)

Astronomical datasets often consist of a grid of datapoints in two or more dimensions, such as images, spectroscopic time-series, and imaging time-series. Gaussian processes (GPs) are a powerful tool for dealing with correlated noise in these data, arising from both astrophysical and instrumental systematics. Despite this, popular GP optimisations such as celerite are largely restricted to 1D datasets. Extensions to 2D data typically come with limited flexibility or poor scaling. As a motivating example, this limitation is significant for transiting exoplanet spectroscopy with JWST, as current analyses typically assume each spectroscopic light curve is statistically independent, despite many potential sources of wavelength-correlation, including detector readout noise and stellar granulation. This introduces a potential bias in atmospheric retrievals which is typically ignored. To solve this problem, we present new, exact GP optimisations implemented in the JAX-based package luas, which extend 1D GP optimisations to multidimensional data using Kronecker product algebra. These GP optimisations are highly flexible, permit efficient analysis of 2D datasets containing millions of datapoints, and in many cases have a cheaper cost than transit light curve modelling. These optimisations are key to pushing the limits of exoplanet spectroscopy to smaller targets around bright host stars, as we increasingly hit instrumental and astrophysical noise floors. We suggest further applications to the analysis of ground-based high resolution spectroscopic time-series, extreme precision radial velocities, and high-contrast imaging, drawing on the connection that certain 2D GP models represent a probabilistic generalisation of PCA. Applications may even extend to machine learning and geostatistics, as we present novel optimisations for the Linear Mixing Model and the Linear Model of Coregionalisation.

4. Does DESI point towards transient cosmic acceleration?

TLDR: Introducing a cubic e-fold source term into an oscillatory dark energy equation of state causes cosmic acceleration to be transient, asymptotically driving the universe toward a static state while remaining consistent with current cosmological observations. (gemini)

We investigate an oscillatory equation of state for dark energy that oscillates around the mean value $-1$ and includes a source term proportional to the cube of the number of e-folds. Due to this source term, the oscillation amplitude shifts towards the $w_{\rm de}-1$ regime. As the equation of state enters the $w_{\rm de}>-1$ regime in the future, the dark energy density dilutes, causing the Hubble parameter to decrease asymptotically towards zero, so that the present acceleration is transient: the Universe transitions to decelerated expansion and approaches a static state as $a\to\infty$, without ever contracting. We compare this equation of state with BellDE, CPL and flat $Λ$CDM. These values are consistent with those of the baseline model, apart from a mild upward shift in $Ω_{\rm m}$ for the Union3 combination. Among all the models, BellDE yields comparatively stronger Bayesian evidence than the other models, with weak evidence for the Pantheon+ compilation and moderate evidence for the Union3 and DES-Dovekie compilations. The sourced oscillatory model yields inconclusive evidence for Pantheon + and Union3, while weak evidence for DES-Dovekie relative to $Λ$CDM.

6. Jeans Dynamical Constraints on the IMF in Massive Quiescent Galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ from JWST/NIRSpec

TLDR: Spatially resolved JWST spectroscopy of massive quiescent galaxies at z ~ 0.7 indicates that bottom-heavy initial mass functions are confined to galaxy cores rather than extended throughout the galaxy, supporting an inside-out growth mechanism driven by minor mergers. (gemini)

We present dynamical masses for nine massive quiescent galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ from the JWST-IMFERNO program, measured from ultra-deep ($\sim32$hr), spatially resolved NIRSpec/MSA spectroscopy combined with NIRCam imaging. The most massive of these galaxies have previously been found to have a bottom-heavy initial mass function (IMF), based on measurements of dwarf-sensitive absorption features. Here, we examine whether the stellar masses inferred for these IMFs are consistent with the dynamical masses of these galaxies. We derive the dynamical masses by including Jeans Anisotropic Modelling within a forward-modelling framework that accounts for instrumental optics, source morphology, and geometric effects. We compare the resulting dynamical masses to the stellar masses for different IMF assumptions. A Milky Way IMF produces physically plausible dynamical-to-stellar mass ratios but implies central dark matter fractions ($f_{\rm DM,1R_e}=0.54\pm0.06$) that are a factor $\sim2$ higher compared to local ETGs and simulations. These results support a scenario where the cores of the most massive quiescent galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ had a bottom-heavy IMF, similar to those observed in the centres of local early-type galaxies. Our results are consistent with an inside-out growth scenario, in which size and mass growth of massive galaxies proceeds after quenching through minor mergers, leaving the bottom-heavy cores mostly intact.

7. Through Ups and Downs: the Transient Lensing Behaviour of SIDM halos undergoing Core Collapse
Frank C. van den Bosch, Yarone M. Tokayer

TLDR: Gravothermal core collapse in self-interacting dark matter haloes produces a non-monotonic evolution in gravitational lensing strength, peaking when the core surface density approaches critical rather than when the halo achieves its highest central density. (gemini)

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes can become extremely dense following gravothermal core collapse, motivating their use as explanations for unusually concentrated gravitational lenses. Using an emulator calibrated on gravothermal fluid calculations, we investigate whether increasing central density necessarily strengthens lensing. We follow initially Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) haloes through core formation and collapse deep into the short-mean-free-path regime, and compute finite-source magnifications, critical curves, and source-plane lensing cross sections for isolated spherical haloes with elastic, velocity-independent self-interactions, including external shear in selected calculations. Lensing strength evolves non-monotonically: it initially decreases during core formation, rises during early collapse, and generally declines at later stages despite continued growth of the central density. This decline accompanies the emergence of a dense inner core whose mass and radius decrease within a more extended, lower-density core. Time-averaged enhancement relative to NFW haloes depends on the lensing statistic, halo concentration, and interaction cross section. Generalized NFW haloes with central cusps $ρ\propto r^{-γ}$ with $γ\geq 2$ always have larger lensing efficacies than core-collapsed SIDM haloes of equal mass and are therefore poor proxies, including in terms of the demographics (size and radial thickness) of arcs and Einstein rings. The core collapse stage at which SIDM haloes are optimal lenses coincides with the epoch at which the characteristic core surface density is close to critical, which typically occurs during the early stages of core collapse. These results demonstrate that central density alone is insufficient to characterize SIDM lensing and that its evolving mass distribution must be modelled explicitly.

9. DarkMatterFM-Registry: A Machine-Readable Registry of Dark-Matter Models Extracted from the arXiv Literature
Ioana A. Zelko, Jo Bovy, Nolan Koblischke

TLDR: Systematic extraction and schema validation of thousands of dark matter theories from the literature demonstrates that the primary bottleneck in testing novel models is a lack of shared computational infrastructure, such as universal Boltzmann solvers and likelihood frameworks, rather than missing theoretical derivations. (gemini)

To use a dark-matter model one needs to know what defines it, which observables it affects, which codes and likelihoods evaluate it, and under what assumptions a published bound applies. That information is scattered across tens of thousands of papers. We present DarkMatterFM-Registry, a machine-readable registry of dark-matter models extracted from arXiv full texts by a staged large-language-model (LLM) pipeline: 6,145 structured model cards, validated against a seven-file JSON Schema suite, ranked by expected value for observational follow-up, and enriched for the top ~2,000 with observables, constraints, and theory-to-observable routes. The model cards, schemas, roadmap, and ranking are released making the project reproducible and the cards available for the community.

10. Primordial non-Gaussianity as a large-scale cosmic-web bias
Kilian Peraza Vera, Francisco-Shu Kitaura, Francesco Sinigaglia

TLDR: Reformulating local primordial non-Gaussianity through large-scale cosmic-web environments defined by an inverse-Laplacian Hessian field recovers the characteristic scale-dependent bias and provides an alternative environmental framework for both generating mock catalogs and measuring inflationary physics. (gemini)

Primordial non-Gaussianity is a unique probe of the physics of inflation, but its large-scale-structure signature is difficult to measure because it appears on the largest, most cosmic-variance-limited modes. The standard view of local-type primordial non-Gaussianity is isotropic: it induces a scale-dependent correction to tracer bias, $Δb(k)\propto f_{\rm NL}k^{-2}$, on large scales. Here we show that the same large-scale response can be reformulated as an anisotropic, nonlocal environmental-bias effect. Motivated by the spectral cosmic-web hierarchy of Kitaura and Sinigaglia, we define a Hessian field $\mathcal{H}_{ij}(\bf{x})\equiv\partial_i\partial_j\nabla^{-4}δ(\bf{x})$, whose trace obeys $\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{H}=\nabla^{-2}δ$ and therefore carries the same $k^{-2}$ enhancement that appears in the Dalal et al. scale-dependent-bias term. We show that these four weights reproduce the large-scale $k^{-2}$ rise of the tracer bias. We further show that existing halo catalogs without PNG can be post-processed to imprint an effective $f_{\rm NL}$ signature. Beyond mock generation, the resulting cosmic-web response motivates an observational statistic based on changes in tracer occupation of large-scale environments relative to a matched Gaussian reference, complementary to the conventional low-$k$ power spectrum.
