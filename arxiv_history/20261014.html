1. Interpreting the stacked kinetic SZ signal II: the degeneracy between satellite galaxies and baryonic feedback
Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea, Boryana Hadzhiyska, Raul E. Angulo

TLDR: At the precision of current DESI$\times$ACT data, neglecting satellites in the modelling biases the inferred gas fractions low, by up to a factor of two, and overestimates the feedback strength, particularly for the LRG and BGS samples. (sumy fallback)

The kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) signal stacked around a galaxy sample traces the gas around halos and is a powerful probe of baryonic feedback. Its interpretation, however, depends on the satellite galaxies in the sample. Using the FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations and DESI-like samples of luminous red galaxies (LRGs), bright galaxies survey (BGS), and emission-line galaxies (ELGs), we show that satellites and feedback have similar effects: both suppress the small-scale signal. This degeneracy arises because stacking on satellites, which are offset from the centre of their host haloes, mimics the extended gas distribution produced by feedback. Although satellites make up only 20-30% of each sample, they account for 50-80% of the signal because they occupy more massive halos than centrals; thus their imprint on the stacked profiles can be as large as that of feedback. At the precision of current DESI$\times$ACT data, neglecting satellites in the modelling biases the inferred gas fractions low, by up to a factor of two, and overestimates the feedback strength, particularly for the LRG and BGS samples. Remaining agnostic about the satellite population avoids this bias but reduces the constraining power, so robust constraints would require knowing the satellite fraction to better than 10%, for instance from lensing and clustering data of the same galaxy samples. Finally, a further contribution from satellites will become statistically significant with upcoming surveys: satellites trace the anisotropic gas distribution around their host haloes, an effect that contributes 10% of the signal, and will likely require simulation-based models to capture.

3. Enabling Polyspectra via Stochastic Realizations of Inverse Decay Curvature Perturbations in Axion Inflation
Matthew A. Reinhard, Zachary Slepian, Atsushi Taruya

TLDR: Depending on the choice of parameter $ξ\in\{2, 2.5, 3\}$, which controls gauge field particle production in this model, our method generates realizations with an average runtime of a few hours on 1 CPU core. (sumy fallback)

Current and upcoming galaxy-redshift surveys offer a promising path for constraining inflationary physics. These constraints are often imposed by relating correlations of the primordial curvature perturbation to the late-time galaxy correlation functions. The primordial correlators can either be computed analytically, or via realizations of the primordial curvature perturbations that are initialized on a grid and evolved numerically according to the classical equations of motion. In this work we take a hybrid approach, initializing the fields in the Bunch-Davies vacuum; and then imposing time evolution through direct numerical integration. Our method efficiently generates realizations of the inverse decay primordial curvature perturbations for axion inflation---an inflationary model in which a pseudoscalar inflaton field couples to a $U(1)$ gauge field via a Chern-Simons interaction. Depending on the choice of parameter $ξ\in\{2, 2.5, 3\}$, which controls gauge field particle production in this model, our method generates realizations with an average runtime of a few hours on 1 CPU core. We compute the power spectrum and bispectrum from these realizations and find a surprisingly large discrepancy between our result and previous work that used an approximation for the gauge field mode functions---up to a factor of $\sim4$ difference for the bispectrum in the case of $ξ=2$. This new method enables not only rapid computation of higher order polyspectra, but also generation of initial conditions for large-scale structure analyses.

7. Untangling Similarities and Differences in Star Formation Histories Across Cosmological Simulations

How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.

10. Neural Bayesian Networks for Modular and Scalable Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

TLDR: They split a joint distribution into local conditional mechanisms arranged on a directed acyclic graph, which makes them modular, interpretable, and ready to perform probabilistic inference. (sumy fallback)

Bayesian Networks (BNs) are established tools for letting agents reason under uncertainty in sequential decision-making. They split a joint distribution into local conditional mechanisms arranged on a directed acyclic graph, which makes them modular, interpretable, and ready to perform probabilistic inference. Their classical formulation, however, has limitations in both expressiveness and scale: each conditional is usually stored as a table or a linear-Gaussian form, and inference evaluates the graph one node and one query at a time. In this paper we introduce Neural Bayesian Networks (NBNs), in which each conditional is a differentiable, tensor-valued mechanism, while the graph and its factorization are unchanged. NBNs retain the modularity and local mechanisms of BNs, can represent any learnable conditional distribution, and run natively on GPUs. We also release NBN, a PyTorch-based library for learning and inference in NBNs, together with a public benchmark. Our evaluation shows gains in both expressiveness and scale: NBNs fit distributions that classical BN libraries struggle to represent, and on large networks and large batches of queries they run two orders of magnitude faster thanks to GPU parallelization. NBN is available at https://github.com/Giovannibriglia/NeuralBayesianNetworks.

Observed GGSL probabilities exceed $Λ$CDM predictions by nearly an order of magnitude, but the relative roles of internal structure and environment remain unclear.
