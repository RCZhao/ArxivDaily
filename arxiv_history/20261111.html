1. An Energy-Based Perspective on Machine Learning
Bo Dai, Yilun Du

Energy-based models (EBMs) define a distribution through a scalar energy, $p_θ(x)\propto\exp(-E_θ(x))$, and are usually presented as one model family among many. This tutorial has a broader goal: beyond introducing EBMs, it uses them as a single lens that connects the different aspects of machine learning. Any EBM is specified by three choices, the energy, which determines what the model represents; the inference procedure, which samples from or optimizes the energy; and the learning method, which fits the energy to data. We first develop these components, including sampling, variational inference, and learning by maximum likelihood, noise contrastive estimation, pseudolikelihood, score matching, and adversarial training, and show that the learning methods share one contrastive structure. We then show that discriminative models, generative models such as GANs, normalizing flows, diffusion and autoregressive models, representation learning methods such as contrastive learning, word2vec, BERT, denoising auto-encoders, and diffusion representations, reinforcement learning with EBM policies and world models, inverse reinforcement learning, and planning, as well as inference-time computation with verifiers, are all EBMs that differ in these three choices, mostly in how they handle the partition function and sampling. This view explains existing methods, organizes them into a common framework, and suggests new algorithms by replacing one component while keeping the others, with composition of energies as a principled way to combine learned models at inference time.

2. Connecting field-level and n-point function measurements of galaxy bias
Hsiang-Ming Huang, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt

TLDR: Our results establish the RG flow as a natural formulation for relating galaxy bias measurements at different cutoffs and connecting the schemes used in field-level and $n$-point function analyses. (sumy fallback)

Precise measurements of galaxy bias parameters are essential for extracting cosmological information from galaxy surveys. Field-level analyses infer bias coefficients at a finite cutoff $Λ$, typically related to the lattice resolution, while $n$-point function analyses typically adopt renormalization conditions defined in the low-$k$ limit. In this work, we use the renormalization group (RG) to relate field-level measurements at different cutoffs and connect them to $n$-point function measurements. We generate mock galaxy fields with a Lagrangian perturbation theory forward model and a galaxy bias expansion, and then measure the bias parameters across a range of cutoffs. We show that the RG predictions describe the measured running and, in the limit $Λ\to 0$, agree with the (tree-level) $n$-point function measurements of first-order $b_δ$ and second-order $b_{δ^2}, b_{{G}_2}$. We also measure the running on halo catalogs based on full $N$-body simulations and show that the RG prediction describes the measurements well within the predicted systematic errors. Our results establish the RG flow as a natural formulation for relating galaxy bias measurements at different cutoffs and connecting the schemes used in field-level and $n$-point function analyses.

4. Less is More: Unbiased Galaxy Clustering from Fewer Merger Trees
Oscar J. Hickman, Sownak Bose, Kai Wang, Carlton M. Baugh, Baojiu Li

TLDR: The required fraction rises with redshift and as the number density falls, is the same for two model variants, and transfers between boxes through the galaxy and pair counts. (sumy fallback)

Galaxy clustering could strongly constrain galaxy formation models but is rarely used, because the two-point correlation function (2PCF) requires a complete galaxy catalogue at every point in parameter space. Semi-analytic models (SAMs) process each halo merger tree independently, so running a SAM on $m$ of $k$ disjoint partitions of the trees, each spanning the full box, cuts the cost by $k/m$. Because a halo's galaxies are kept or discarded together, one-halo pairs are diluted less than two-halo pairs, and the naive estimate overstates small-scale clustering by a factor $k/m$. We introduce the Sparse and Unbiased Clustering Estimator (SaUCE), which removes this bias with weights set by $m$ and $k$ alone. It has no free parameters, gives exactly unbiased pair counts, and applies to any catalogue built from a random subset of whole haloes and to any pair-count statistic. The required fraction rises with redshift and as the number density falls, is the same for two model variants, and transfers between boxes through the galaxy and pair counts. The scatter, derived in closed form, is strongly correlated between bins, so a likelihood must use the full covariance. A recipe for choosing the fraction makes the 2PCF affordable for model calibration and simulation-based inference.
