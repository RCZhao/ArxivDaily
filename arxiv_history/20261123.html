1. Fast and accurate large-volume Lyman-$α$ forest mocks with three-dimensional U-Net trained on IllustrisTNG and FLAMINGO

The Lyman-$α$ forest probes matter structure at $z\gtrsim2$, constraining baryon acoustic oscillations, neutrino masses, and dark matter properties. Interpreting current and upcoming spectroscopic surveys requires mock catalogues spanning Gpc-scale volumes while capturing small-scale intergalactic medium physics. This combination of high resolution and large volume is prohibitively expensive for hydrodynamical simulations. We present a field-level emulator, based on a three-dimensional U-Net, that maps approximate density and velocity fields directly to Ly$α$ optical depth and transmitted flux. At $z\sim3$, we train in small volumes and test on much larger, independent ones in two settings: IllustrisTNG (training on a $110\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ box and testing on a $300\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ box with 2LPT and $N$-body inputs) and FLAMINGO (training on $1\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ and testing on $2.8\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ with $N$-body inputs). Within each suite, the emulator reproduces $P_{\rm 1D}$ to $2.8\%$ in TNG and $5.6\%$ in FLAMINGO, the bispectrum to $4$--$5\%$, and the trispectrum to $10$--$16\%$, while also recovering the flux PDF and broad correlation structure. Field-level emulation thus provides a practical route to large-volume Ly$α$-forest mocks for current and future surveys.

2. Information-Criterion-Driven, Non-linear Deprojection of Angular Imaging Systematics for the SPHEREx Galaxy Clustering Sample
James R. Cheshire, Sean T. Bruton, Charuhas W. Shiveshwarkar

TLDR: For effects whose spatial patterns are known or well-modelled \emph{a priori}, a common mitigation approach regresses (deprojects) the density variation associated with a known angular template. (sumy fallback)

Imaging systematics that inject false, or suppress true, galaxy clustering signals are of primary concern for current- and next-generation galaxy surveys, such as the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx) in its search for local primordial non-Gaussianity $f_{\rm NL}$. As statistical errors shrink with growing galaxy samples, systematic rather than statistical uncertainties come to dominate the cosmological error budget. For effects whose spatial patterns are known or well-modelled \emph{a priori}, a common mitigation approach regresses (deprojects) the density variation associated with a known angular template. In this work we present and demonstrate novel aspects of this procedure as will be applied to the SPHEREx galaxy clustering sample. The method automatically selects a template set of minimal-but-sufficient complexity while limiting the number of templates actually regressed, minimising cosmological information loss and making modern additive-plus-multiplicative approaches tractable. We demonstrate these features on SPHEREx-like lognormal mock catalogues with injected contamination of various spatial profiles, amplitudes, and levels of complexity. Principled, signal-preserving deprojection of the kind demonstrated here is a crucial step towards achieving SPHEREx's goal of $σ(f_{\rm NL}) < 0.5$.
