1. Joint analysis of the Macquart relation and the dispersion measure power spectrum: cross-covariance and redshift self-calibration
Amy Wayland, Robert Reischke

TLDR: Finally, we show that the redshift distribution of FRBs without identified hosts, which enters every DM power spectrum, can be reconstructed by inverting their DMs with the jointly fitted mean relation without biasing the inferred parameters, whereas a 20 per cent error in the mean relation biases the DM power spectrum by up to 2$σ$ per band for $5\times 10^5$ FRBs. (sumy fallback)

The dispersion measures (DMs) of fast radio bursts (FRBs) trace ionised baryons along the line of sight, probing baryonic feedback. Two complementary observables can be constructed from an FRB catalogue: the DM-redshift (Macquart) relation of host-identified FRBs, and the angular power spectrum of the DM field of the full catalogue. Their joint analysis requires their cross-covariance. We derive it within the halo model and show it is a super-sample effect: only the monopole of an individual DM correlates with the angular power spectrum, yielding an exact, model-independent bound on the correlation coefficient. We validate the calculation against log-normal forward simulations of $10^4$ FRBs. For individual DMs, the correlation with the bandpowers is at the per cent level. Neglecting the cross-covariance changes the width of the $χ^2$ distribution by only 0.8 per cent, so the two observables can be treated as effectively independent for wide-area surveys. In a joint forecast with an LSST-like weak lensing survey, adding the Macquart relation tightens constraints on the physical baryon density by 32 per cent and on the mean host contribution by a factor of 4.1, while improving baryonification parameters by only about 5 per cent, indirectly through the host contribution. Finally, we show that the redshift distribution of FRBs without identified hosts, which enters every DM power spectrum, can be reconstructed by inverting their DMs with the jointly fitted mean relation without biasing the inferred parameters, whereas a 20 per cent error in the mean relation biases the DM power spectrum by up to 2$σ$ per band for $5\times 10^5$ FRBs.

We present a multi-probe observational analysis of a clustering tachyonic Dirac-Born-Infeld~(DBI) dark energy model. Its noncanonical kinetic term gives a subluminal sound speed, so dark energy perturbations can grow on subhorizon scales. Working in a multifluid framework that tracks both the background expansion and linear perturbations, we constrain the model with nested sampling, using Planck 2018 compressed CMB distance priors, DESI DR2 BAO, cosmic chronometers, $fσ_8$ growth data, and three Type Ia supernova compilations: Pantheon$+$, DES-SN5YR, and Union3. 3. Confirming HSC strong lens candidates with DESI Spectroscopy. II. Analysis of 80 galaxy-scale systems with lens redshifts above 0.8

TLDR: Our analysis nearly triples the number of known galaxy-scale strong lenses at z_lens > 0.8, making statistical studies of the total mass structure of high-redshift galaxies possible for the first time. (sumy fallback)

The total mass distribution of galaxies provides a sensitive probe of the fundamental physical processes governing galaxy evolution, including gas inflow/star formation, AGN/SN feedback, galaxy merging, and the nature of dark matter. State-of-the-art cosmological simulations have therefore made extensive predictions for the evolution of the total-mass density slope. Interestingly, their predictions diverge significantly at high redshift, primarily due to differences in how these key physical mechanisms are implemented. However, measurements of the total-mass structure evolution have been largely confined to low and intermediate redshifts, leaving the regime where model predictions differ most significantly (z>0.8) largely unexplored. One system lacking reliable lensing features in current imaging data is classified as a non-lens. Our analysis nearly triples the number of known galaxy-scale strong lenses at z_lens > 0.8, making statistical studies of the total mass structure of high-redshift galaxies possible for the first time. This expanded sample provides an unprecedented opportunity to test the predictions of cosmological simulations and discriminate between competing physical prescriptions.

4. Probing the Seed Effect of Supermassive Primordial Black Holes with Weak Gravitational Lensing

We investigate the cosmological implications of supermassive primordial black holes (PBHs) as a component of dark matter (DM), focusing on their seed effect on large-scale structure formation. In contrast to the Poisson effect associated with the discrete distribution of PBHs, the seed effect arises because PBHs act as early gravitational seeds, accreting surrounding DM and developing dense DM spikes to form dressed PBHs. These structures enhance the matter power spectrum, particularly on small scales. We model their contribution within the halo-model framework under two prescriptions for DM accretion: an optimistic scenario (Case 1) and a conservative scenario (Case 2), which predict different modifications to the 1-halo term. We constrain the PBH abundance using weak-lensing measurements from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3), performing a Markov Chain Monte Carlo analysis to derive upper limits on the PBH fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$, as a function of the PBH mass $M_{\rm PBH}$. We find that weak lensing places competitive constraints on supermassive PBHs, with the upper limits on $f_{\rm PBH}$ becoming progressively stronger toward higher PBH masses. Case 1, which predicts more efficient DM accretion and hence a larger enhancement of the matter power spectrum, yields stronger constraints than Case 2; in particular, it excludes $f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{-4}$ for $M_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{10} M_\odot$. We further forecast the sensitivity of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) using mock weak-lensing data and find that the constraints could improve by approximately two orders of magnitude. Our results establish weak gravitational lensing as a sensitive probe of the seed effect of supermassive PBHs and demonstrate its potential to complement other cosmological and astrophysical searches.

Sound waves are expected to be a dominant source of gravitational waves (GWs) from cosmological first-order phase transitions. Their spectrum is commonly predicted using the fluid velocity two-point correlation function under the Gaussian approximation.
