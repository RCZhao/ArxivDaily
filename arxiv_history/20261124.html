3. Redshift-space 3$\times$2pt statistics of galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments at one-loop order
Toshiki Kurita, Atsushi Taruya, Teppei Okumura

TLDR: We compare both models with power spectra measured from halos in $N$-body simulations and find good agreement on mildly nonlinear scales. (sumy fallback)

Intrinsic alignments of galaxies, long regarded as a systematic in weak lensing, are now recognized as a cosmological probe complementary to galaxy clustering. Exploiting them with spectroscopic surveys requires accurate models in redshift space beyond the linear regime. In this work, we derive the galaxy density--shape cross-power spectrum and shape auto-power spectra at one-loop order in redshift space. We consider two models, one based on the effective field theory (EFT) and the other on the Taruya--Nishimichi--Saito (TNS) prescription. They share the bias expansion but differ in how they treat small-scale velocities along the line of sight. The EFT model expands the effects of these velocities perturbatively and absorbs their sensitivity to small scales into counterterms. We show that, for the sky-projected shape field, this model requires six renormalized bias parameters and two counterterms. Up to order $k^2$, the stochastic terms require two parameters for the cross spectrum and four parameters for the auto spectra. The TNS-based model keeps the one-point velocity dispersion in an unexpanded damping factor and treats it as a single free parameter. We extend our previous linear-bias TNS-based model of the cross spectrum to nonlinear bias and the shape auto spectra. We find that all loop integrals reduce to those already used for shape spectra in real space and galaxy clustering in redshift space, and we evaluate them with FFTLog. We compare both models with power spectra measured from halos in $N$-body simulations and find good agreement on mildly nonlinear scales. These models provide a basis for joint cosmological analyses of galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments with imaging and spectroscopic galaxy surveys, in which intrinsic alignments are utilized as a cosmological probe.

End-to-end uncertainty propagation in scientific inference often requires repeated evaluation of coupled simulators spanning multiple physical domains, often at prohibitive computational cost. Global 21-cm cosmology is an extreme example: a millikelvin cosmological signal must be separated from radio foregrounds and instrumental effects four orders of magnitude larger, making inference acutely sensitive to model misspecification. 6. Neural-network-powered new-physics searches by predictive goodness of fit
Jing Lei

TLDR: On established one-dimensional and five-dimensional (dilepton) benchmarks, the false-positive rate always stays within Monte Carlo error of nominal across different nuisances and the learners' tuning. (sumy fallback)

Model-independent searches for new physics test whether data agree with a Reference model with unknown nuisance parameters. Because the shape and location of a signal are not specified, these tests call for flexible learners (fitting procedures such as neural networks), and a likelihood ratio maximized over a neural network falls outside the standard asymptotic theory, making the calibration a major challenge. We propose a predictive alternative: the departure from the Reference is fitted on part of the data and its likelihood gain over the fitted Reference is evaluated on the rest, cross-fitted over several folds to preserve sample efficiency. The nuisance estimation error is propagated into the variance of the test statistic to first order, in the spirit of Neyman's C(alpha) test, so that the test need not be recalibrated at each nuisance value. The randomness in the fitted departure is treated by a central limit theorem for cross-validated statistics under algorithmic stability, plus a variance correction when stability is not achieved. The calibration is analytic, and no pseudo-experiments are needed. On established one-dimensional and five-dimensional (dilepton) benchmarks, the false-positive rate always stays within Monte Carlo error of nominal across different nuisances and the learners' tuning. The test's power relative to existing learned tests is mixed: higher for some signals and lower for others, depending on how well the fitted departure captures the signal.
