1. Tests of equivalence principle violation in doubly coupled dark energy
Yannick Kegel, Ziyang Zheng, Luca Amendola

TLDR: Extending coupled dark energy with a nonminimal gravitational coupling introduces an observable parameter for cosmological weak equivalence principle violation, which future galaxy surveys like SKA Phase 2 can tightly constrain. (gemini)

Recent work has shown that a simple form of coupled dark energy with a constant coupling produces a good fit to the DESI data. In this scenario, dark matter, but not baryons, couples to a scalar field, thereby violating the weak equivalence principle (EP). We first generalize the coupled dark energy model by adding a nonminimal coupling between the scalar field and gravity, and dub the resulting model "doubly coupled dark energy". We then construct a parameter $E_P$ in this model that quantifies EP violation on cosmological scales and can be measured with large galaxy surveys in a model-independent way. Finally, we forecast constraints on the couplings for present and future spectroscopic galaxy surveys, namely DESI, Euclid, and SKA Phase 2 (SKA2). To keep the analysis as general as possible, we leave the initial power spectrum shape and the galaxy bias free to vary in several bins. Among these surveys, SKA2 could provide the tightest constraint on the coupling strength in the coupled dark energy scenario, with a relative $1σ$ uncertainty of $22\%$. The constraints weaken when both couplings are varied, but can be further improved by increasing the number of galaxies detected.

2. Model-Independent Assessments of Tidal Alignment and Density-Weighting Dependence in Galaxy Intrinsic Alignments
Paul Rogozenski, Sukhdeep Singh, Jonathan Blazek, Rachel Mandelbaum, Hung-Jin Huang, Ananth Tenneti

TLDR: Hydrodynamical simulations and observational data reveal that density-weighting primarily amplifies galaxy intrinsic alignments on large scales, while central galaxies maintain a linear response to underlying tidal fields well into the 1-halo regime. (gemini)

Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are an important source of systematic uncertainty in weak gravitational lensing measurements and also serve as a test for models of galaxy formation and evolution. These galaxy alignments are usually modeled assuming that the IA are sourced by the tidal fields around the galaxies. However, galaxy properties, like their proximity to other galaxies and their mass, provide alignment contributions beyond the tidal field. In this work, we perform novel model-agnostic tests to determine the impact of density-weighting on the IA of galaxies using data from the IllustrisTNG cosmological hydrodynamical simulation and SDSS-III (BOSS). We show that the primary effect of density-weighting is an increase of the alignment amplitude at large scales, whereas the small-scale impact is sample-dependent. We also assess the tidal alignment paradigm for IllustrisTNG galaxies using two-point correlation functions of galaxy shapes, tidal fields, and the sampled background tidal field of the simulation box. We find, given the precision of our measurements, that central galaxies align linearly to the underlying (non-linear) tidal field well into the 1-halo regime.

4. Two-loop matter power spectrum from FFTLog and kite integrals
Paola Cappellazzi, Johannes M. Henn, Marko Simonović

TLDR: Combining FFTLog decomposition with a Mellin-Barnes representation enables highly accurate, millisecond evaluations of the two-loop matter power spectrum in the Effective Field Theory of Large-Scale Structure. (gemini)

We present a fast and accurate method for computing the two-loop matter power spectrum in the Effective Field Theory of Large-Scale Structure (EFTofLSS). Using FFTLog, we decompose the linear power spectrum into complex power laws, reducing the two-loop corrections to cosmology-independent massless Feynman integrals of kite topology. A central numerical challenge is the evaluation of these integrals for complex powers with large imaginary parts, where the highly oscillatory integrands make accurate numerical evaluation particularly challenging. We overcome this difficulty using a Mellin--Barnes representation summed over a uniform lattice, achieving approximately ten digit precision even for large imaginary powers, with an error estimate for each integral. The resulting precomputed matrices allow the two-loop power spectrum to be evaluated for a new cosmology in a few milliseconds. Comparing with direct numerical integration, we find agreement at the $10^{-4}$ level relative to the power spectrum up to $k=0.2$ $h$Mpc$^{-1}$ after accounting for EFTofLSS counterterm contributions and FFTLog decomposition errors. The same framework extends to biased tracers and redshift-space distortions, providing the building blocks for general two-loop calculations in EFTofLSS. We make the code for evaluating the kite integrals publicly available, together with precomputed matrices and an implementation for the two-loop matter power spectrum as a concrete application. Our results can be directly incorporated into existing FFTLog-based codes for large-scale structure, extending their current one-loop capabilities to two loops within the same computational framework.

We present a new rescaling and stacking method to test cold dark matter predictions through the dynamical modelling of galaxy groups using the Jeans equation. We test the viability of this method on idealised, line-of-sight group data at $z=0$ in the IllustrisTNG-300 simulation, using subhalos and satellite galaxies as tracers. Dynamical mass profiles are measured using the code `Gravsphere' for seven distinct quality cuts, three on the dark matter substructure and four on the `visible' satellite galaxies. This suggests dynamical systematics may still be present in the data, which will need to be quantified in future work before application to surveys.

6. Numerical evaluation of one-loop integrals using quadrature rules

TLDR: Formulating one-loop Feynman integrals within the causal Loop Tree Dual representation allows for a numerical quadrature method achieving stretched exponential convergence for Euclidean kinematics. (gemini)

We introduce a numerical quadrature algorithm with stretched exponential convergence to evaluate one-loop integrals in dimensional regularisation in the causal Loop Tree Dual (cLTD) representation. The key feature that makes this possible is the fact that for Euclidean kinematics all singularities of the cLTD integrand lie on a compact set in loop momentum space. We prove that our algorithm admits a stretched exponential convergence with the number of integrand evaluations, and we illustrate it by evaluating triangle, box and pentagon integrals at selected Euclidean kinematics points. 7. FOMO-Z. Optimal galaxy classification, photometric redshifts, and reconstruction of population distributions
G. F. Lesci

TLDR: A new open-source probabilistic framework, FOMO-Z, utilizes empirical reference catalogs and flexible color selections to deliver computationally efficient, distribution-free photometric redshift estimation and galaxy classification. (gemini)

We present FOMO-Z (Flexible Optimizer of Multi-dimensional Operators and photo-$z$s), a set of statistical methods implemented in Python for galaxy classification and probabilistic inference from photometry. The methods use a reference sample with known galaxy properties. They rely on explicit, physically interpretable criteria and make no assumptions about the functional form of the color distributions. Binary selections are calibrated using either linear color conditions, particularly suited to background galaxy selection, or flexible color--color grids, seeking to maximize completeness given a threshold on purity. For probabilistic inference, the code identifies reference objects with compatible colors within photometric uncertainties to estimate each test galaxy's probability of satisfying user-defined criteria, as well as probability density functions of continuous properties such as redshift. The code also reconstructs property distributions of selected galaxy populations using photometrically similar galaxies in the reference sample. This framework supports prior weights and reference redshift probability distributions. A built-in feature-selection method identifying informative colors is also introduced. We demonstrate applications to background galaxy selection, galaxy population classification, tomographic redshift calibration, and photometric redshift estimation. The code achieves results consistent with those obtained through machine-learning approaches, such as self-organizing maps, while remaining computationally efficient, making it suitable for large photometric surveys and future Stage IV cosmological analyses. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/GiorgioLesci/FOMO-Z.

8. When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation
Daniela Breitman

TLDR: The suspiciousness statistic successfully identifies model misspecification in cosmic dawn and reionization inferences, detecting internal tensions even when flawed forward models deceptively pass standard chi-squared goodness-of-fit tests. (gemini)

As observations of the cosmic dawn (CD) and epoch of reionisation (EoR) rapidly improve in precision, the approximate forward models used to interpret them might struggle to keep pace. Bayesian inference with a misspecified model, one that cannot represent the processes generating the observations, can yield tightly constrained, yet misleading and biased astrophysics, with different parts of the observations favouring incompatible parameter values. Model misspecification can evade a $χ^2$ goodness-of-fit test, and Bayesian model selection requires a better model, which is not always available. We therefore apply the suspiciousness, a statistic developed to quantify tensions between datasets, to CD/EoR inference as a model criticism tool that tests whether these parts can be explained by the same parameters. We first apply it to HERA 21-cm power spectrum (PS) upper limits, which are too weak to reveal misspecification, as expected, and to HST and JWST UV luminosity functions (LFs). Under our galaxy model, the UV LFs at $z\leq10$ and $z>10$ are in tension at $5.0σ$ in the Gaussian limit, a failure visible in the fits, although doubling the uncertainties of the $z>10$ observations reduces it to $2.1σ$. Finally, we analyse a mock SKA-Low AA$^\ast$ 1080 h 21-cm PS with the model that generated it and with a simpler, misspecified model. We find that the misspecified model silently fails: it fits the PS well, passing visual inspection and, for most noise realisations, a $χ^2$ test, yet its inferred astrophysics is wrong. The Bayesian evidence disfavours it, but only relative to the true model, which is unavailable for real observations. The suspiciousness finds no tension for the well-specified model, but a $\sim3.4σ$ tension for the misspecified one, strongly ruling it out without requiring a more adequate candidate model.
