We present a method to calculate likelihood-based upper confidence limits on axion-photon coupling under this circumstance. We compare the likelihood-based method conceptually and quantitatively with tailor-made methods, i.e. the one proposed by Brubaker et al. In particular, we point out the correspondence of BPM to e-values, proposed as an alternative to conventional p-values in statistics.

2. How To Build A Modern Diffusion Language Model
Volodymyr Kuleshov, Marianne Arriola, Yair Schiff, Gilad Turok, Guanghan Wang

TLDR: This sequential process leaves parallel GPU hardware underutilized during inference--a bottleneck that has grown acute as progress shifts toward scaling post-training and inference-time compute--and commits irreversibly to each token without the ability to revise earlier mistakes. (sumy fallback)

The frontier of large language model (LLM) performance has advanced rapidly in recent years, fueled by scaling laws for pretraining on large text corpora. However, mainstream LLMs have fundamental limitations: they generate text autoregressively, producing outputs sequentially from left to right one token at a time. This sequential process leaves parallel GPU hardware underutilized during inference--a bottleneck that has grown acute as progress shifts toward scaling post-training and inference-time compute--and commits irreversibly to each token without the ability to revise earlier mistakes. Discrete diffusion models address these limitations by generating tokens in parallel, starting from a noisy or masked sequence and iteratively refining the entire output over a series of denoising steps. This parallel generative process enables speeds of thousands of tokens per second, provides a new axis of inference-time compute scaling, and supports built-in error correction and controllable generation. Over the past two years, discrete diffusion has scaled to billions of parameters across biological sequence modeling and natural language, with open-weights and commercial releases from leading labs. This article provides a survey of key ideas in discrete diffusion and a high-level introduction to how modern diffusion LLMs are built, covering their foundations, core algorithmic and architectural building blocks, applications in biology and language, and implications for future LLM scaling laws.

6. FastPM: Fast Pretrained Model for Cosmological Simulation-Based Inference

TLDR: These results demonstrate that transfer learning from fast simulations is a simple and powerful route to high-fidelity SBI, and highlight the promise of simulation-based foundation models in cosmology. (sumy fallback)

Modern cosmology increasingly relies on large ensembles of expensive simulations to model the non-linear Universe and extract information from upcoming surveys. However, generating high-fidelity simulations---whether gravity-only N-body or full hydrodynamical---requires substantial computational resources. In this work, we demonstrate that transfer learning from fast low-fidelity simulations can dramatically reduce the cost of training machine learning models, in particular for simulation-based inference (SBI). We train a Fast Pretrained Model (FastPM) on a set of Fast Particle-Mesh (FastPM) simulations to infer $Ω_m$ and $σ_8$ from the power spectrum, and finetune on a smaller set of high-fidelity CAMELS N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. Remarkably, the gains persist for the total matter power spectrum of full hydrodynamical simulations: although FastPM contains no baryonic physics, pretraining on FastPM runs that together cost less than a single hydrodynamical simulation still reduces the total training simulation compute by a factor of $\sim$5. Moreover, pretraining on more accurate FastPM runs yields more accurate posteriors than training on CAMELS alone achieves with any number of simulations we consider. These results demonstrate that transfer learning from fast simulations is a simple and powerful route to high-fidelity SBI, and highlight the promise of simulation-based foundation models in cosmology.

An application to gene-expression inference illustrates the practical value of the framework for uncertainty-aware scientific inference.
