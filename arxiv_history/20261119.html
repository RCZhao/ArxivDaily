1. Deconstructing the cosmic baryon distribution with fast radio bursts
Koustav Konar, Yin-Zhe Ma, Léon V. E. Koopmans, P. Daniel Meerburg

TLDR: Coupling fast radio burst dispersion measures with foreground galaxy survey data enables the precise mapping of diffuse cosmic baryons and gas fractions across voids, filaments, and halos, demonstrating that ionized gas predominantly resides in low-density environments. (gemini fallback)

The precise distribution of baryons in the Universe remains an open question in cosmology, as their highly diffuse nature hinders direct observation. However, mapping it is integral for precision cosmology in studies of gravitational lensing, galaxy evolution and the cosmic microwave background with current and upcoming surveys. We use fast radio bursts (FRBs) and their dispersion measures (DMs) to probe and localise this baryon distribution in the large-scale structure (LSS). Using IllustrisTNG simulations, we classify the LSS into eight bins of increasing matter density, analogous to voids, filaments, and haloes. We model the DM contribution from each substructure and compare it with the simulated DMs to infer the density estimator, the ratio of electron number to total matter density. The resulting overdetermined system is solved in a Bayesian framework with a simulation-physics-informed model. To demonstrate observational applicability, we create a Euclid-like mock galaxy catalogue and build a density estimator based on luminous matter along FRB sightlines. We apply a bias correction for the underlying dark matter distribution and compare the two density estimators to recover the electron number density across the eight bins. Electrons are predominantly localised in low-density regions, while the ensemble mean across all bins aligns with the global theoretical estimate. Combining the FRB and galaxy density estimators, we recover the density of ionised baryons as $Ω_b f_{ion} = 0.0455^{+0.0028}_{-0.0026}$ (simulation: 0.0462), corresponding to $Ω_b = 0.0479^{+0.0029}_{-0.0027}$ for $f_{ion}=0.95$. Our bin-wise electron density estimate reproduces the simulation ground truth to within 10 per cent and gives gas fractions of $0.186 \pm 0.012$, $0.179 \pm 0.015$ and $0.046 \pm 0.014$ in voids, filaments and haloes, against ground truths of 0.167, 0.173 and 0.067.

2. Thermodynamic Parameterizations of Dark Energy in Light of DESI DR2 BAO

(gemini fallback)

The physical origin of cosmic acceleration remains one of the central unresolved problems of modern cosmology. Although the $Λ$CDM model successfully describes a wide range of cosmological observations, its interpretation in terms of a strictly constant vacuum energy faces long-standing theoretical challenges, motivating the search for physically grounded alternatives. In this work, we develop a thermodynamic parametrization of dark energy (TPDE) by combining the second law of thermodynamics with the kinematic evolution of the Universe. By imposing physically motivated constraints on the cosmic deceleration parameter and reconstructing its evolution through a Padé approximant, we derive a one-parameter extension of $Λ$CDM in which late-time accelerated expansion emerges from thermodynamic principles rather than an assumed cosmological constant. We constrain the model using Planck 2018 CMB temperature, polarization, and lensing observations, DESI DR2 BAO measurements, and the Pantheon$+$, DES-Dovekie, and Union3 Type Ia supernova compilations. Despite maximum-likelihood fits comparable to $Λ$CDM, Bayesian model comparison consistently yields a weak-to-moderate preference for TPDE. These results demonstrate that thermodynamic reconstruction provides a simple, physically motivated, and observationally competitive alternative to $Λ$CDM, offering a framework for investigating the origin of dark energy with forthcoming high-precision cosmological observations.

3. LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA stochastic constraints on first-order phase transitions: A tale of priors

We re-examine the methodology for constraining cosmological first-order phase transitions from gravitational-wave searches using Bayesian statistical analysis. Inspiralling supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHBs) likely contribute to at least part of the signal, but there may be additional cosmological sources from new physics in the early universe. 9. A twist in the tail: GWLENS, a fast and accurate tool for wave-optics lensing of gravitational waves

Gravitational-wave lensing in the wave-optics regime requires evaluating the Fresnel--Kirchhoff diffraction integral, whose integrand is highly oscillatory and must be evaluated numerically, as closed-form solutions exist only for a few lens models. We present a novel, efficient algorithm that splits the integration domain at a point beyond the region containing the lensed images. The bounded head region is integrated along the real axis with Gauss--Legendre quadrature, while the semi-infinite tail is rotated into the complex plane, where the integrand decays exponentially, and is evaluated with Gauss--Laguerre quadrature.
