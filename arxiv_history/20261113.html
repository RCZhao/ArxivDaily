We present the pipeline design of the photometric redshift processing function for Data Release 1 (DR1) of the $Euclid$ mission, and the validation and performance of our cosmology-related products that are released as part of DR1-Foundation. The $Euclid$ mission aims to constrain the equation-of-state parameter of dark energy, $w$, to percent-level accuracy through the measurement of large-scale structure via weak gravitational lensing and galaxy clustering. As part of DR1-Foundation we release determinations of photometric redshifts (photo-$z$), corrected photometry, and object classes that are used to form the basis of the cosmological samples. The release furthermore includes estimates of the spectral energy distributions of stars and galaxies for the careful modelling of the point-spread function for weak-lensing measurement. We estimate the performance of the photo-$z$ point estimates and probability distribution functions using overlapping spectroscopic redshifts, which are weighted to match different flux-limited target samples. We find that the current performance is somewhat below mission design requirements ($40\%$ in scatter and a factor of 2 in outlier fraction) at the nominal survey depth ($I_{\rm E}<24.5$), but closely matching requirements at slightly brighter magnitudes ($I_{\rm E}<24$), consistent with pre-launch analyses and the slightly shallower external data (up to $0.3\,\mathrm{mag}$) with respect to end-of-mission expectations. Data from Rubin-LSST will help alleviate this issue by the time of the final cosmology analyses. The other cosmology-related outputs are tested to the extent that is possible with currently available comparison data. This paper is partnered by an article describing the measurement of the physical properties of galaxies for the purpose of non-cosmological science.

The Euclid mission is revolutionising the study of strong gravitational lenses by providing high-resolution imaging over an unprecedented survey area, increasing the number of known lenses by two orders of magnitude compared to previous surveys. Characterising this sample of lens-galaxies requires accurate measurements of their physical properties. However, standard catalogue photometry often blends the light from the foreground lens and the lensed background source, limiting the reliability of derived galaxy parameters. We present a homogeneous catalogue of photometric redshifts and physical properties for 9538 galaxy-scale strong lenses identified in Euclid's DR1 strong-lens sample. We use deblended multiwavelength photometry obtained from the lens modelling of Euclid and ground-based images with PyAutoLens. The photometric redshifts of the lens galaxies are estimated using the template-fitting code Phosphoros. The stellar masses, SFRs, stellar ages, and metallicities are inferred through SED fitting with Prospector, adopting a flexible non-parametric SFH and a Salpeter IMF. The robustness of the inferred parameters is assessed through comparisons with spectroscopic redshifts and external galaxy surveys. The sample of lenses spans a broad redshift range of $0.13\lesssim z_{\rm phot} \lesssim 1.98$, with a median $z_{\rm phot} = 0.68^{+0.33}_{-0.29}$. The sample is dominated by massive quiescent galaxies with a $M_\ast$ range $[10^{10},\,10^{12.1}]\,M_\odot$ and a median $\log_{10}(M_\ast/M_\odot) = 11.39^{+0.29}_{-0.33}$. This catalogue represents one of the first large-scale characterisations of strong-lens galaxies in the Euclid era. By combining deblended photometry with homogeneous photometric redshift and SED-fitting analyses, it provides a foundation for studies of galaxy evolution, lens-galaxy scaling relations, and the connection between stellar and total mass in strong lenses.
